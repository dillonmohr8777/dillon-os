### Monday, June 16: Thought leadership (Maher El-Abdallah)
**Format:** Native text post (optional 60–75 sec talking-head video)
**Topic:** AI readiness isn't a software purchase

Most CHRO conversations about AI still start with tools. Which vendor. Which license. Which pilot.

The harder question comes first: Is your HCM data clean enough to trust? Do your managers know how to interpret what the system surfaces? Do you have governance for decisions that affect pay, promotions, and performance?

AI amplifies what you already built. Strong process, clear ownership, and accurate payroll history get better. Fragmented data, shadow spreadsheets, and unclear roles get worse, faster.

Before you greenlight another demo, ask your team three things:

• What decision are we trying to improve, not just automate?
• Who owns the outcome when the model is wrong?
• What does our post-go-live support model look like when AI adds a new layer?

Implementation leaders don't need hype. They need a realistic path from stabilization to transformation. That's the work.

### Friday, June 19: Thought leadership (Joann Scolaro, CPP)
**Format:** Carousel (5 slides) or native text post
**Topic:** The 90-day window after go-live

I've managed enough HCM implementations to know the pattern.

Week one: celebration. Week four: quiet panic. Week twelve: either a stabilized operation or a team running on heroics.

The difference usually isn't the software. It's whether anyone planned for what happens after the consultants leave.

Three signals you're in the danger zone:

• Payroll corrections are handled in side spreadsheets "just until we fix it"
• The same three people answer every manager question
• Leadership treats support tickets as noise instead of data

You don't need a bigger internal team on day one. You need a support model that matches your maturity: stabilize first, then essentials, then acceleration.

Implementation isn't a project with an end date. It's an operating discipline.

### Bonus: Tuesday, June 17 (Barbara Tonelli)
**Format:** Native text post
**Topic:** Process clarity before platform change

Teams often ask me how to "get ready" for a new HCM platform. My answer is boring on purpose.

Document who owns each process today. Not who should. Who actually does.

• Who approves time-off exceptions?
• Who reconciles payroll before transmission?
• Who owns job data changes for hourly vs. salaried populations?

If those answers live in people's heads, the platform won't fix it. It'll expose it on go-live week.

Clarity before configuration. Always.
