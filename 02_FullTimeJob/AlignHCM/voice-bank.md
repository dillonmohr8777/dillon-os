# Align HCM LinkedIn Voice Bank

Five distinct author voices for LinkedIn content. Never mix voices inside one post. Each section includes the POV, signature themes, posting cadence, and do/don't rules.

---

## Maher El-Abdallah -- CEO

**POV:** Visionary, strategic, industry-level thinker. Has seen hundreds of HCM decisions play out. Speaks from authority, not from credentials.

**Signature themes:**
- What leadership gets wrong about HCM investment
- The human side of payroll and workforce decisions
- Industry trends and what they mean for HR leaders
- Payroll implementation philosophy (what I tell every client)
- AI in HR -- opportunities and limits
- Culture as competitive advantage

**Cadence:** Mondays (primary slot). Additional thought leadership when relevant.

**Formats:** Talking-head video scripts (60-90 sec), native text posts, long-form thought leadership.

**Voice characteristics:**
- Speaks like a founder and peer, not a vendor
- Opinionated and willing to say what others won't
- Direct. Short paragraphs.
- Uses "we" for Align HCM but "I" when sharing personal perspective or stories
- Occasionally conversational and warm, but never fluffy

**Sample opening lines:**
- "Most HCM implementations fail before configuration starts."
- "The question isn't which platform to pick. It's whether you're ready to use it."
- "I've sat in hundreds of post-go-live calls. Here's what I never say at the start of one."

**Do not:**
- Use buzzwords (leverage, synergize, holistic, transformative)
- Sound like a press release
- Overexplain concepts his audience already knows
- Use em dashes

## Barbara Tonelli -- Operations / HR

**POV:** Operational. Process-focused. The person who actually makes HR work at scale. Practical, not theoretical.

**Signature themes:**
- HR process design and standardization
- High-performance culture (how it's actually built, not how it's described)
- Manager effectiveness and people operations
- Benefits administration and compliance fundamentals
- Internal mobility as a retention tool
- Change management in HCM transitions

**Cadence:** Distributed throughout the month. Additional posts as scheduled.

**Formats:** Native text posts, carousels, static graphics.

**Voice characteristics:**
- Grounded and practical
- Speaks from experience, not from frameworks
- Comfortable with nuance ("it depends" when it actually does)
- Uses specific examples and numbers when available
- First-person perspective, collegial tone

**Sample opening lines:**
- "High-performance culture doesn't start at the retreat. It starts at the first bad behavior that leadership ignores."
- "Your onboarding process is a statement of what you actually value. Not what's in the handbook."
- "HR spends too much time administering and not enough time designing. That gap compounds."

**Do not:**
- Sound like a LinkedIn thought leadership template
- Use vague culture-speak without specifics
- Use em dashes

## Joann Scolaro, CPP -- Sr. Project Manager

**POV:** People-focused implementation expert. Has managed complex go-lives and knows where projects go wrong. Practical wisdom, not theoretical advice.

**Signature themes:**
- Project management inside HCM implementations
- What makes a go-live succeed vs. struggle
- Stakeholder alignment and change management
- Payroll accuracy and compliance discipline
- The human side of system transitions (employees and admins)
- Lessons from the field

**Cadence:** Mondays (rotation with Maher). Additional posts throughout the month.

**Formats:** Talking-head video scripts, native text posts.

**Voice characteristics:**
- Warm but direct
- Leads with lessons, not processes
- Uses "we" for implementation team, "you" for client
- Comfortable admitting complexity
- Storytelling-adjacent (sets a scene, names the dynamic, draws a lesson)

**Sample opening lines:**
- "Two weeks before go-live is not the time to discover your time rules weren't configured."
- "The best thing you can do for a new system is train the people who hate change first."
- "Parallel payroll is not optional. It's the one place where you prove the work."

**Do not:**
- Sound like a PM methodology document
- Over-formalize the voice
- Use em dashes

## Moe El-Abdallah -- Technical Implementation

**POV:** The platform expert. Knows UKG, Dayforce, ADP at a configuration level. Speaks to admins, technical HR leads, and implementation consultants.

**Signature themes:**
- Platform configuration tips (UKG, Dayforce, ADP Workforce Now)
- Integration architecture between HCM and HRIS/finance/benefits
- Common configuration mistakes and how to fix them
- Reporting and analytics setup
- System admin best practices
- Implementation technical workstreams

**Cadence:** Distributed throughout the month.

**Formats:** Native text posts, short carousels (tip lists), static graphics.

**Voice characteristics:**
- Technical but readable
- Numbered tips and steps work well in this voice
- Specific: names modules, features, settings where relevant
- Avoids oversimplifying but also avoids jargon without context
- Short sentences, dense information

**Sample opening lines:**
- "Your UKG pay rules are only as good as your policy documentation."
- "Integration failures during cutover are almost always data problems, not connector problems."
- "Three things to configure before you invite employees into a new HCM system."

**Do not:**
- Sound generic (this voice only works when it's specific)
- Write at the awareness level -- this audience already uses these tools
- Use em dashes

## Cross-Voice Rules

- One voice per post. No mixing.
- No em dashes in any voice.
- No starting sentences with: And, But, Or, It is, Do not, That is, This is.
- Use contractions in all five voices.
- Bullet character for lists. Never dash bullets.
- Always check: does this sound like something this specific person would say?
