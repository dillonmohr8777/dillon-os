## Monday, June 9, 2026: Thought Leadership

**Author:** Maher El-Abdallah (CEO)
**Format:** 60 to 90 second talking-head video script
**Dimensions:** Native video (record vertical or square for mobile feed)

### Hook (on screen text)
Go-live was months ago. Why does it still feel like implementation?

### Video script

Your HCM platform went live. Payroll runs. Employees get paid.

So why does your team still feel like they're in implementation mode?

Most organizations treat go-live as the finish line. The real work starts the week after.

Configuration drift. New hires who never got proper system training. Reporting requests that pile up because nobody owns the backlog. A support model that defaults to calling the consultant from the project.

That's not a staffing problem. It's a support model problem.

At Align HCM, we built SmartCare around four maturity stages: Stabilize, Essentials, Accelerate, and Transform. Mid-year is when you find out which stage you're actually in.

If you're six months post-go-live and still firefighting payroll exceptions every cycle, you're not failing. You just haven't stabilized yet. And that's fixable with the right expert access.

Before you budget for another implementation partner or another internal FTE, run an honest health check on three things: ticket volume trends, report backlogs, and how long fixes take to close.

Where does your team land today? I'd like to hear it in the comments.

## Friday, June 13, 2026: Personality / Thought Leadership

**Author:** Joann Scolaro, CPP (Sr. Project Manager)
**Format:** Carousel (7 slides, 1080x1350)
**Theme:** Implementation burnout signals and what PMs can do about them

### Carousel outline

**Slide 1: Cover**
Headline: Your go-live date is set. Your team is exhausted.
Subhead: 5 burnout signals I watch for on every HCM implementation
Visual: Navy background, orange accent bar, Joann headshot lower right

**Slide 2**
Headline: Signal 1: Decisions live in Slack threads with no owner
Body: When the same question gets asked three times in three channels, the team isn't confused. The decision log is.

**Slide 3**
Headline: Signal 2: Testing windows shrink but scope doesn't
Body: Cutting test cycles doesn't save time. It moves risk to the first live payroll cycle.

**Slide 4**
Headline: Signal 3: SMEs get pulled into daily firefights
Body: Your payroll lead can't configure earning codes and answer help desk tickets in the same week. Something has to give.

**Slide 5**
Headline: Signal 4: "We'll fix it after go-live" is the default answer
Body: That phrase is a scope decision, not a schedule workaround. Name it early or pay for it later.

**Slide 6**
Headline: Signal 5: Nobody can articulate in vs. out of scope
Body: If the team can't answer "is that in scope?" in under 10 seconds, burnout is already in motion.

**Slide 7: CTA**
Headline: What PMs can do this week
Bullets:
• Protect two-hour focus blocks for SMEs
• Document every decision in one shared log
• Escalate resource gaps before they become payroll risk
• Say no with a reason, not a shrug
Footer: Joann Scolaro, CPP | Align HCM
