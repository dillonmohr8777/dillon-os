## Corrections to carry into the room

**1. "Pb&J reporting" is PBJ — Payroll-Based Journal.** The September call note
called it product surface and said "neither is the marketing argument." That is
the most consequential error in the file. The August audit had it right; the
September note regressed because the term was transcribed phonetically.

Verified chain, all confirmed 2026-09-10:

| Link | Fact |
|---|---|
| Statutory basis | **ACA §6106** requires LTC facilities to submit direct-care staffing data "based on payroll and other auditable data," quarterly |
| Filing mechanics | Deadlines Feb 14 / May 15 / Aug 14 / Nov 14; spec **4.10.0 mandatory 1 Apr 2026**; 22.5 hrs/employee/day edit; FQ4 moved to iQIES 17 Aug 2026 |
| Public rating | PBJ drives the **Five-Star staffing domain**: RN HPRD, total nurse HPRD, weekend staffing, RN turnover, total nurse turnover, administrator turnover |
| **Money** | **SNF VBP withholds 2% of Medicare FFS Part A and redistributes 60%.** From **FY2026** the measure set includes **Total Nurse Staffing** and **Nursing Staff Turnover** — both PBJ-derived |
| FY2027 set | SNFRM, SNF HAI, DTC-PAC, Long-Stay Hospitalization, Nursing Staff Turnover, Total Nurse Staffing, Discharge Function Score, Falls with Major Injury. FY2028 adds SNF WS PPR |
| Turnover update | Turnover now defined on a **90-day gap** (was 60), affecting FY2026–FY2027 thresholds |

Home health: HHVBP went nationwide via the CY2022 HH PPS final rule, **±5%**
payment adjustment. CY2026 larger-volume cohort weights: OASIS 40% / claims
40% / **HHCAHPS 20%**, with Potentially Preventable Hospitalization 15%,
DTC-PAC 15%, MSPB-PAC 10%, Overall Rating 10%, Willingness to Recommend 10%.

**So the claim is one step, not three:**

> Your payroll system produces the file that determines two of the eight
> measures CMS uses to redistribute a 2% withhold of your Medicare Part A
> revenue, and the staffing stars on the page your referral sources read.

That is a finance and compliance purchase, mechanically, with no inference. It
moves the buyer from HR Director to **CFO / VP Finance / Administrator**, and
the content from "engagement" to **filing accuracy, HPRD math, turnover
definitions, star-rating exposure.**

**2. The 2024 minimum staffing rule is dead — the August audit still cites it
as current.** 3.48 total nurse HPRD / 0.55 RN / 2.45 aide / 24-7 RN was
**vacated 7 April 2025** (N.D. Tex., *AHCA v. Kennedy*, major-questions
doctrine), given a **ten-year enforcement moratorium** in the July 2025
reconciliation act, and **formally repealed by CMS interim final rule
3 December 2025**, reverting to the prior 8-consecutive-hours-per-day RN
standard. Publishing content that treats 3.48 as a requirement would be a
credibility-ending error in front of this company.

The sophisticated version, and a good interview line:

> The staffing **mandate** died. The staffing **measurement** didn't, and it's
> the part that carries money now.

**3. Say HPPD, PBJ, Five-Star and SNF VBP — not readmission rates.** Empeon's
own content runs staffing → PBJ → Five-Star → referrals and revenue. Their blog
states it: *"PBJ errors aren't just a reporting issue — they can directly
impact a facility's Five-Star rating"* and *"in early 2025, more than 5% of
U.S. nursing homes were penalized in a single quarter for failing to fully or
accurately report PBJ data, with 801 facilities dropping to a one-star staffing
rating in January alone."* Turnover-data errors lock a facility into the lowest
score for six consecutive quarters, and turnover is **half** the staffing
component. Readmissions and satisfaction do drive CMS reimbursement broadly,
but **Empeon does not make that link publicly.** Use their vocabulary.

**4. The "9,000+ healthcare organizations" figure is NOT Empeon's.** It appears
in the *About Apploi* boilerplate of the June 2024 Apploi/Empeon partnership
release, and Viventium's homepage carries the identical claim. Empeon's own
boilerplate in that release has no client count at all.

**Empeon's real citable numbers: 20M+ paychecks annually, 97.5% client
retention (homepage), 630,000+ users (Skilled Nursing News 2025-12-09).**

**5. The 98.5% renewal rate is not found anywhere public.** Homepage says
**97.5% client retention**; the pricing page says **"less than 1% churn"**
(implying ~99%). Two adjacent pages, materially different claims. Use 97.5%
cited to the homepage, or "the high nineties." The *inconsistency itself* is a
legitimate, gentle observation — exactly what a marketing manager notices.

Also: "98.5% renewal means the product isn't the problem, so growth is purely
acquisition" assumes retention is measured on logos, not revenue. Better:
*you keep essentially everything you win, so the entire growth constraint sits
upstream of the win.* Same conclusion, no assumption.

**6. The 65% white-paper attribution is unverifiable externally.** The ebook
library is real and unusually buying-stage (8 operator-grade titles), but
whether the ebooks are gated could not be confirmed — no crawler reaches the
form, and no "raise my hand" language or consult offer was found anywhere in
the flow. **Make Nack say it again** (Q3 below).

**7. Shift swap is table stakes, not state of the art.** It exists — the Oct
2023 scheduling release quotes Rapoport on *"shift swap capabilities with HR
approval,"* and the scheduling page offers an OT-cost preview at the approval
step, which is genuinely good. But it is **absent from their own pricing-page
feature list**, and **SmartLinx markets mobile shift swap as its own headline
differentiator.** The defensible version is the **all-in-one integration**:
swap approval that can see the payroll consequence, because scheduling,
timekeeping and payroll share one employee record.

## The regional concentration, and how to say it out loud

The Orthodox-community concentration **cannot be verified as stated from public
sources**, but circumstantial evidence converges from five independent
directions. The strategic read is right; the framing must be different.

**Hardest evidence, from Empeon's own site:** the pricing page lists, under
Payroll, **"Home Care Wage Parity Calculations"** and **"Spread of Hours
Calculation."** Wage parity is NY Public Health Law §3614-c, applying only to
Medicaid home care in NYC, Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester. Spread of hours is
a NY State DOL rule. **A company built for the national market does not ship
those first.**

Supporting: HQ Montebello adjoins Monsey/Suffern; customer principals cluster
in Cedarhurst and Williamsburg; the engineering req pairs **Airmont NY with
Greater Lakewood NJ**, not a normal engineering talent-market pairing. Case
studies: WeCare Centers (13 SNFs NY/PA), FreedomCare (~20,000 weekly caregiver
paychecks, CDPAP), Limestone Fiscal Services (back office for 30+ healthcare
companies), Vibrant ABA (Williamsburg). Logos resolve to Anchor Health Homecare
(Brooklyn), Edison Home Health (Brooklyn, acquired by Help at Home 2022), Hill
Valley (39 facilities, 6 states).

**What it does not prove:** proportion — eight logos and four case studies are
a marketing selection, not a census. Help at Home is Chicago-based, PE-backed,
$1B+, and breaks the pattern entirely. And nothing here establishes anyone's
religious affiliation; it is name- and address-based inference.

**The framing to use — fully sourced, no claim about anyone's religion:**

> *"Your public references cluster hard in New York-metro skilled nursing and
> home care — closely-held operators, several running out-of-state facility
> portfolios — and the product computes wage parity and spread of hours, which
> are New York statutes. Meanwhile the site, the blog and the Platinum
> sponsorship at FHCA all read as generic national healthcare HCM. That's a
> company with a deep regional book trying to sell nationally, and those two
> things are not yet talking to each other."*

The entire go-to-market problem in three sentences.

**One landmine: do not raise Edison.** Searching "Edison Home Health Care"
surfaces a DOJ/EDNY release about Brooklyn home health agencies settling fraud
claims for $9.75M. Whether Edison is a party was not verified. Nothing good
comes from mentioning it.

## Why the expansion is hard: four things that were free

The community market and the general market are not the same market at
different scales. They are different **mechanisms**. Inside the community, four
things cost nothing:

1. **Distribution** — reputation travels through shared institutions with no
   media budget.
2. **Underwriting** — a referrer's vouching substitutes for proof. Nobody asks
   for SOC 2 or three reference calls.
3. **Qualification** — referrals arrive pre-sorted.
4. **Retention** — churn carries a social cost, not just a switching cost.

A hand-raise motion sitting on top of a ~98% keep rate is not a marketing
engine. It is an **order-taking counter attached to a referral engine.** The
white paper is probably the *conversion* surface for demand word of mouth
already created. That distinction has almost certainly never been separated in
their data, and separating it is the highest-value first analysis available.

> Outside the community, the white paper stops being a conversion surface and
> has to become an acquisition surface. Nobody has ever asked it to do that job.

**The two symmetric failure modes, worth naming out loud:**

- **Identity-bounded ceiling** — the brand stays legible only to the origin
  community; general-market buyers read it as "not for us."
- **Base erosion** — the company scrubs its origin to look generic, loses the
  referral density that produced the retention, and still doesn't beat ADP on
  brand. **This is the worse outcome**, because it destroys the asset that was
  working.

**What actually exports.** Community credibility does not transfer. Three
things do: operational proof at segment depth (wage parity, live-in pay,
per-visit pay, blended OT, differentials, multi-rate, mixed W-2/1099,
credential-gated pay, 24-hour ops, multi-facility allocation); **a regulatory
filing capability** (PBJ); and benchmark data from a dense customer base.

The claim has to shift from *who they serve* to *what they have solved*:

> Not "we're the standard in our community." Not "we're healthcare payroll" —
> too broad, that's ADP's word. **"Empeon is built for the payroll that
> post-acute operators actually run — and it files your PBJ."**

Test: **can a Phoenix-based 4-facility SNF chain read the proof and see
itself?** That means case studies described by operator **shape** (setting, bed
count, states, worker mix, pay complexity) rather than operator **name**. Shape
travels. In a concentrated customer base, names do the opposite of travel.

## The demand math nobody has said out loud

From the authenticated Semrush pull (Aug 2026): healthcare payroll software
**320/mo**, home care payroll software **260**, PBJ reporting requirements
**70**, PBJ reporting software **50**, skilled nursing payroll software **~10**.
The biggest adjacent term, home care scheduling software, is **1,300**. Total
realistic intent across the whole portfolio is low thousands per month.

**You cannot build a general-market lead-gen engine on that volume.** This is
the strategic point that connects everything: inside the community distribution
was free; outside it, **search will not replace it, because the search demand
does not exist at scale.** SEO here is a credibility and capture layer, not a
growth engine. Growth must come from **manufactured distribution** — outbound,
associations, events, partners, LinkedIn — pointed at a small, enumerable
universe of operators.

## Competitors — and the sharpest single fact

**Viventium bought Apploi.** Apploi was Empeon's own announced strategic
partner (June 2024). **Viventium acquired it 2026-01-30**, plus Perks4Care,
closing the loop from application → credential → onboarding → schedule →
timecard → paycheck. Empeon has an ATS, but reviewers complain the API is
restricted. **That is the "why does this role exist, why now" answer**, and
raising it demonstrates more market awareness than anything else available.

**Viventium** is the closest analog by a wide margin. Founded 1991 in Brooklyn
(768 Bedford Ave, Williamsburg) by the **Kohn family** as BDB Payroll;
post-acute-exclusive HCM; now Berkeley Heights NJ. **218 employees (+13% YoY),
~$28.8M revenue, 15,600 LinkedIn followers** — roughly 2× Empeon's headcount,
4× its following. **PE-backed: majority growth investment from LLR Partners,
Feb 2023.** CEO Navin Gupta; SVP Marketing Matt Resteghini. Owns wage parity /
CDPAP / Medicaid / live-in pay / blended OT language, SNF page with PBJ.

Its origin story — Brooklyn, family-founded, post-acute, NY compliance depth —
is **structurally identical to Empeon's.** It is the mirror image that took
outside capital and went national. Both the threat and the roadmap.

**Netchex** is the real content adversary: owns the exact-match pages *and*
writes the "best of" list defining the category. It publicly claims to be
**"the only HCM platform that generates CMS-compliant PBJ files natively"** —
and Empeon's own payroll FAQ already says it has built-in PBJ, HPPD and wage
parity. **A falsifiable competitor claim Empeon can contest today with a
product it already ships**, sitting unused because `/solutions/reporting/`
doesn't mention PBJ in its title, H1 or FAQ.

**SmartLinx** — Hackensack NJ, 80–90 people, est. $30–40M, ~3,400 workplaces.
Markets *"predict Five-Star Ratings"* and mobile shift swap. Nack's former
employer. Reviewers flag complex implementation, first-payroll errors.

**The generalists — ADP, Paychex, Paylocity, Paycom, UKG, Dayforce, Paycor —
are the actual displacement targets**, because payroll is Empeon's mandatory
entry module (stated twice in their own FAQs: **every deal is a payroll
rip-and-replace, not a bolt-on**). SelectHub head-to-head: ADP wins on breadth
and integrations; **Empeon wins on healthcare specificity, ease of use, support
responsiveness and price.**

**Adjacent:** OnShift (listed as an Empeon *integration*; weak reviews, 3.6
support — a competitive opening), HHAeXchange (owns EVV→payroll and split
payments in home care), WellSky, MatrixCare, PointClickCare, Netsmart.

**Gig marketplaces (ShiftKey, Nursa, Clipboard, IntelyCare)** are not
competitors — they are the **expensive pain** Empeon's scheduling should
reduce. "Cut agency spend" is a better shift-swap message than "swap shifts
easily," and their own podcast data supports it: *"more than 20% of skilled
nursing labor hours now come from agency staff."*

**Where Empeon wins, and the reviews are genuinely strong.** SoftwareAdvice
**4.8/5 across 46 reviews** (ease of use 4.91, value 4.89, support 4.71).
SelectHub **98% recommend across 56 reviews** — higher than Viventium (94%) and
ADP (85%). Capterra "Best Ease of Use 2025." Recurring praise: **a single named
support team that knows your account** rather than a ticket queue. Customers
switched TO Empeon because the previous vendor was *"hard to get a hold of."*
Support responsiveness is the wedge.

**Where Empeon loses:** reporting and customization limits (most repeated
complaint — cannot lock filters, no lookback reports); **API limitations**
named repeatedly as an integration ceiling; **no native PointClickCare
integration**; heavy implementation; **support-team rotation "leading to a lack
of continuity,"** which directly undercuts their strongest selling point; "lots
of small fees."

**And the acquisition problem in one metric: 56 total reviews versus
Viventium's 109 and ADP Workforce Now's 13,337. 3,733 LinkedIn followers versus
Viventium's 15,600.** In a category where buyers shortlist from review sites
and peer referral, **Empeon is nearly invisible outside its existing network.**
That is the most defensible thing to offer to fix.

**No "Empeon vs X" comparison pages exist on their own site.** SelectHub,
SoftwareFinder and SoftwareAdvice own that entire keyword surface. Given that
vendor-selection queries are exactly what won in the Align work, that is a
concrete, immediately actionable gap.

**Capacity signal: ~9 blog posts in 9 months, with a complete gap between
June 8 and September 3, 2026.** For a company whose best acquisition motion is
content, that is why this role exists.

## The 90-day plan

Solo operator role. No team to build. Reports into a cross-functional revenue
team with VP Sales and RevOps.

**Days 1–30 — Instrument and correct. Ship nothing new.**

1. **Separate the 65%.** Of the hand-raisers, how many were *created* by the
   paper vs *referred and then converted* by it? Split by referral source,
   geography, prior touch. Until that is split, the "proven owned-channel
   motion" claim is unproven for the general market. Highest-value analysis in
   the plan; takes a week.
2. **Define marketing-sourced pipeline in HubSpot** before spending a dollar —
   first touch, lead creation, assist, opportunity source, revenue source,
   immutable original-source fields, full UTM coverage. Literally the role
   brief's six-month outcome, and Dillon has done it before (1,479 deals
   audited; the $54K Weissman deal correctly reclassified once deterministic
   tracking started 07-17).
3. **Fix the two credibility leaks:** mobile performance (sampled 26–41
   Lighthouse; homepage and payroll **LCP >14s**) and the stale 3.48 HPRD claim.
4. **Baseline the hand-raise SLA.** Prior work found a **41% follow-up gap**. A
   hand-raise nobody calls is not a lead.

**Days 31–60 — Claim the PBJ category.**

5. **Rebuild `/solutions/reporting/` as a PBJ page.** CMS language, §6106, spec
   4.10.0, job-code mapping, XML generation, audit trail, quarterly calendar.
   Directly contest the Netchex "only HCM platform" claim.
6. **Ship the HPPD/HPRD + star-rating exposure calculator.** Takes the
   operator's own census and hours; shows HPRD, weekend staffing, turnover on
   the 90-day definition, and where they sit against SNF VBP thresholds. The
   linkable asset, the sales tool, the conference demo and the hand-raise
   magnet in one object — and it works precisely *because* it uses the
   prospect's data rather than a fabricated benchmark.
7. **Two exact-match pages:** `/industries/skilled-nursing/payroll-software/`
   and a canonical home care payroll page owning wage parity with NY as proof
   state.
8. **Weekly LinkedIn franchise** in the CFO's language: filing deadlines, spec
   changes, turnover definition mechanics, VBP threshold movement.

**Days 61–90 — Manufacture distribution, prove one repeatable path.**

9. **Proof engine on operator shape, not name** — 3 case studies by setting,
   bed count, states, worker mix.
10. **Competitive kit** — Viventium and Netchex switch stories, with a source
    registry so every claim is dated (blank cell = "not confirmed," never
    "competitor lacks it").
11. **One paid search test**, entered *after* tracking exists. Zero paid
    keywords today against CPCs of $56.45 (healthcare payroll software) and
    $36.23 (healthcare HR software) — small volume, high intent, cheap to
    learn on.
12. **Associations and events as the distribution substitute** — the
    replacement for free word of mouth, and the only channel that scales
    against a small enumerable universe.

**The 90-day success criterion is not lead volume.** It is **one instrumented,
repeatable acquisition path outside the community, with a defensible
sourced-pipeline number attached.** Given a ~98% keep rate, proving one
repeatable path is worth more than a spike.

## Seven questions for Nack

1. **"When your PBJ file goes out on the 14th, is Empeon the system of record
   for that filing, or does the operator reconcile it somewhere else first?"**
   Tests whether PBJ is a real product or a checkbox, and signals immediately
   that you know what PBJ is and that it is the strategic asset.
2. **"Netchex publicly claims to be the only HCM platform that generates
   CMS-compliant PBJ files natively. Your own payroll FAQ says you have
   built-in PBJ. Is that claim contestable, and has anyone contested it?"**
3. **"Of the 65% who come in through the white paper — how many of those
   accounts had a prior relationship or a referral before they downloaded
   it?"** The best question in the set. Goes straight to whether the
   acquisition motion is real or a conversion surface on top of referral.
4. **"What breaks when a competitor tries to run a wage-parity or live-in
   payroll for the first time?"** Asks them to describe their own moat
   operationally. The answer tells you whether the depth claim is real.
5. **"97.5% retention with no contracts is extraordinary. Is that number as
   strong outside your core customer concentration as inside it?"** Raises the
   community-transfer question respectfully without asserting the premise.
6. **"The whole search category is small — healthcare payroll software is about
   320 a month, PBJ reporting software about 50. What's the plan for demand
   that search can't supply?"** Separates you from candidates who pitch "a
   content engine."
7. *(If rapport is good)* **"The staffing mandate was repealed in December. Did
   that change how your customers talk about staffing, or did the VBP measures
   keep the pressure on?"**

Plus the org question: **how do you and Morris split growth, and who does this
role report to?**
