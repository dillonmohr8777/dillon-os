# CDPAP, the SEO gap, and what the market actually looks like

Second half of the 2026-09-10 research. Companion to
[[02_FullTimeJob/Empeon/2026-09-10 - Nack briefing and the expansion problem]].

## The CDPAP consolidation — the single most important business fact

**New York legislated ~600 CDPAP fiscal intermediaries out of existence and
replaced them with one.** SFY 2024-25 Enacted Budget amended Social Services
Law §365-f(4-a); Public Partnerships LLC (PPL) selected 2024-09-30; **effective
2025-04-01 PPL is the only authorized fiscal intermediary in New York.**
~280,000 consumers and an estimated 250,000–400,000 personal assistants had
roughly three months to re-register. Program cost reported at $9–11B/yr; the
state projected ~$1B savings and a cut in FI admin cost from ~8% to ~3%.

**The operator damage is documented, not inferred:**

- CDPAANYS executive director Bryan O'Malley: some providers lost **up to 50%
  of their business**; layoffs and in some cases closures.
- **Albany Med Health System Visiting Nurses lost 61% of its revenue.**
- **Addus HomeCare (Nasdaq: ADUS) exited New York entirely**, selling its NY
  personal care business. CEO Dirk Allison: *"It wasn't a stable environment."*
- **Attending Home Care Services** (Brooklyn) attested in a sworn affirmation
  it would lose **$33M gross revenue and $1.5M annual profit**, ~720 consumers
  across ten counties, 20 admin staff.
- **~80,000 of the 280,000 consumers left the program entirely** (NYS DOH).
- The **US DOJ sued NYSDOH, Medicaid Director Bassiri and PPL on 2026-06-16**,
  alleging the scheme caused "substantial harm to… small- and medium-sized New
  York businesses who were put out of business."
- PPL separately agreed to a **$162M wage class-action settlement** covering
  ~200,000 personal assistants (preliminary approval 2026-07-01).

**Empeon's direct exposure is documented through its own flagship case study.**
FreedomCare — Empeon's published customer — was, per its own November 2024
Article 78 petition, **the largest fiscal intermediary in New York with ~30,000
CDPAP consumers**, and stated it "will be forced to close down its CDPAP
business in New York entirely." It sued the state and lost. Limestone Fiscal
Services, a second published case study, is also a fiscal-services entity.
Empeon's homepage claims 20M+ paychecks annually; FreedomCare alone at ~20,000
weekly is on the order of ~1M/year.

**Empeon has published NOTHING about CDPAP.** All 124 post URLs grepped for
"cdpap," "fiscal," "new-york," "wage-parity" — **zero posts.** Viventium *did*
publish CDPAP transition guidance and captured that search demand.

**What is NOT verified: any Empeon revenue loss, churn or layoffs from this.**
Do not assert it. The market facts are verified; the impact is not.

**Safe framing:** *"New York consolidated ~600 CDPAP fiscal intermediaries into
one, effective April 2025, affecting ~280,000 consumers. Two of your four
published case studies are fiscal-intermediary businesses. Whatever the revenue
impact was, the demand-gen implication is clear — that's a market where your
ICP just contracted structurally, which makes expansion urgent rather than
optional."* Accurate, sourced, and it lets them tell you what happened.

**The strategic reading:** this is not "a hard year in New York." A single
legislative act removed an entire customer *category* from one state in one
quarter, and a vendor concentrated in one state, one program and one referral
community absorbed it undiluted. **National expansion here is not an upside
story. It is a covariance problem.** That framing is worth more in an interview
than any growth argument.

## The SEO gap — measured today, 176 URLs parsed

**Empeon's entire site is 176 indexable URLs. 106 of them are blog.** The
commercial layer that has to rank for money terms is 23 URLs.

**Checked twelve buying-stage SERPs. Empeon appeared in exactly one.**

| Query | Empeon | Who owns it |
|---|---|---|
| HPPD / hours per patient day | **#2** | myamericannurse #1, Empeon #2 |
| home care payroll software | absent | GetApp, CareSmartz360, CareVoyant |
| skilled nursing scheduling software | absent | **Viventium #1, Smartlinx #2** |
| caregiver scheduling software | absent | CareSmartz360, Alora, ShiftCare |
| PBJ reporting software | absent | Inovalon, OnShift, Smartlinx |
| urgent care payroll/HR | absent | **Netchex #1, ADP #2, Paylocity #4** |
| hospice workforce mgmt | absent | GetApp, Ciphr, **Netchex** |
| **Empeon alternatives** | **absent from its own brand SERP** | six third-party directories |

**Lead with that last row.** Empeon does not rank on any page of its own
"alternatives" SERP — SelectHub, GetApp, G2, alternatives.co, softwareworld and
research.com decide what a buyer sees when they go looking for a reason to
leave. And on `Empeon reviews`, **#1 is Glassdoor employer reviews** — nine
reviews about what it's like to work there — ahead of Capterra's 4.8/46 buyer
reviews.

**Why the one ranking page ranks.** The HPPD post has a question-form heading
(`What is HPPD?`), a definition, then the arithmetic. Google pulls that
sentence verbatim. The caregiver-scheduling guide, which doesn't rank, is
twelve H3s of benefit labels with no question and no extractable answer. **Same
site, same authority, same author — the difference is structure.** That is the
strongest proof point in the audit: the fix is not "get more authority," it is
"write the other 105 pages the way you already wrote that one."

**Netchex is the one to study and it is not on anyone's competitor list.** 90

in these healthcare-vertical SERPs at all. **The enterprise HCM names are not
the competitive problem. The specialists are.**

|---|---|---|---|

| Payroll (all verticals) | ~500 | unpriced | 3–18 |

**The scheduling query space is ~5× the payroll query space at half the
difficulty — and scheduling is the product Empeon just launched.** Their
sitemap contains `scheduling-beta-launch-press-release`. Matt named shift swap.
The demand data and the product roadmap agree, and the content reflects
neither. Empeon's scheduling page currently has an H1 that reads **"Caregiver"**
— a truncated string. They are not losing a fair fight; they have not entered
it.

### Technical faults gating all of it

- **Four of eight industry pages carry another vertical's title, meta
  description and og:title.** `/industries/urgent-care-centers/` and
  `/rehabilitation-centers/` both say *"HCM Software for Senior Living
  Facilities."* The hospice page does not contain the word "hospice" in its
  title, description or og:title — and Empeon ranks for no hospice term.
  `/industries/skilled-nursing/` is done correctly. **The template exists;
  applying it to four pages is a half-day.** Highest ROI item in the audit.
- **77% of title tags (135 pages) end in `- Healthcare Human Capital
  Management` instead of the brand.** A Rank Math default never configured.
- **The homepage schema declares two different companies** — two `Organization`
  nodes sharing the identical `@id`, one named "Healthcare Human Capital
  Management" (declared first, Facebook only), one named "Empeon" (LinkedIn
  only). Invalid, and it is why the AI surface is thin: **there is no
  consolidated entity for a model to cite.**
- Three self-serving 5-star `Review` nodes on the homepage pointing at Empeon's
  own Organization — outside Google's review-snippet guidelines. Flagging as
  risk, not a manual action.
- **`/pricing/` has no H1.** Neither do six other pages.
- **Four test pages live, indexable and in the sitemap:** `/test/`,
  `/calculator-test/`, `/hubspot-testing/`, `/book-demo-utm-wpforms/`.
- **Duplicate caregiver-scheduling post** cannibalising the 590/mo KD-7 term.
- **`robots.txt` contains invalid directives** — full URLs where paths are
  required, so they block nothing, while the page they target is in the sitemap
  and returns 200.
- **`UA-198153439-2` still firing** — a Universal Analytics property that
  stopped processing data in July 2023. Three years of measurement debt.
- **The HubSpot form is embedded twice on every ebook page** — identical form
  ID, two 538px iframes. A duplicate-embed defect on the primary conversion
  asset.
- **Google Ads conversion tag `AW-11095814509` is live** — they ARE running
  paid, contradicting "zero paid keywords." Two Meta pixels also firing.

### Publishing cadence has collapsed

**2024: 70 posts. 2025: 44. 2026: 10** (through early September). If one slide
says "here is the opportunity," it is this.

## Empeon's own words on how it grows

From `empeon.com/referral/`, verbatim: *"Empeon's partners and clients are our
greatest advocates! **Many of our new clients come through word-of-mouth
referrals.**"*

**This is the most important line in the entire audit.** The company publicly
states its acquisition engine is referral-driven. That is precisely the thing an
"expand beyond the stronghold" mandate has to replace.

**And the hiring confirms it.** Four open roles: one engineer, three
implementation/support. **Zero marketing, zero SDR/BDR, zero national sales.**
That is what a referral-fed company hires — delivery capacity, because leads
arrive without a funnel. Ask whether the marketing role is unposted or whether
you would be the first move.

## Market structure — is this long-tail or concentrated?

**Long tail, decisively — and the tail thickness differs enough by segment that
one GTM motion would misfire.**

~14,700 nursing facilities · ~12,200 Medicare-certified home health agencies ·
6,700 hospices · 32,200–41,500 assisted living communities. Call it
**66,000–75,000 licensed sites**, plus 26,000 non-medical home care agencies
(HCAOA).

**The single largest operator in any segment controls 2–4% of its own segment.**
Ensign, the #1 SNF operator, holds ~2.3% of US nursing facilities. Brookdale,
the largest AL operator, ~1.5% of communities.

SNF concentration, from CMS affiliated-entity data (Aug 2026):

| Chain size | Entities | Facilities | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent (no entity) | — | 4,574 | **31.1%** |
| 2–5 | 139 | 564 | 3.8% |
| 6–29 | 395 | 4,648 | 31.6% |
| 30–99 | 67 | 3,286 | 22.4% |
| 100+ | 9 | 1,618 | 11.0% |

**~76 accounts (the 30+ facility chains) reach a third of the market** — a real
enterprise motion. But **66.5% of facilities sit in chains of 2–29 or no chain
at all.** Hospice averages ~300 patients per provider; home health ~340
patients per agency; assisted living 33–41 beds with 57% of communities under
26 units.

**GTM conclusion: marketing-led inbound as the primary motion, with a named-
account enterprise overlay confined to roughly 200–350 accounts** — the ~76 SNF
chains with 30+ facilities, the ASHA 50 / Argentum 150 senior living operators,
and a short list of home health/hospice roll-ups.

Segments differ in *direction*, which matters for sequencing: **SNF is
contracting and consolidating** (15,297 → 14,518 Medicare-participating since
2019), favoring enterprise. **Hospice and home care are proliferating and
fragmenting** — hospice added 1,866 providers in five years, virtually all
small for-profit entrants — favoring self-serve.

**Why the tail buys:** 5.4M direct care workers, median annual earnings under
$26,000, **46% median nursing home turnover** (MedPAC 2024, PBJ-derived), 9.7M
projected job openings 2024–2034. Every one of those 66,000+ sites has the same
acute recurring staffing problem — the condition under which small operators
self-educate and self-serve rather than waiting for a rep.

## Corrections and cautions

- **Traffic: do not say 32.6K.** Semrush said 21K on 2026-08-28 and 32.6K on
  09-09 — a 55% jump in twelve days, which is a database refresh, not growth.
  Say *"Semrush estimates 21–33K depending on refresh; I'd want Search Console
  to say what it actually is."* The honest answer is the stronger one.
- **Do not use Chobani as a crossover precedent.** Ulukaya's community business
  was Euphrates feta, which barely broke even. Chobani was mainstream
  distribution from day one. He *abandoned* the ethnic niche. Someone will know.
- **Kosher certification is a general-market asset, not a liability.** Mintel
  (2009, n=2,500): top reasons for buying kosher are food quality 62%,
  healthfulness 51%, safety 34% — versus religious observance 14%. Direction
  only; the study is old and unreplicated.
- **The best crossover mechanic is Hebrew National's:** recast the community
  constraint as *exceeding* the general standard. "We answer to a higher
  authority" never argues kosher is good for Jews — it argues kosher law forbids
  what federal regulation permits. Saffron Road ran the same play on halal and
  ended up **~85% non-Muslim, ~25,000 stores including 4,300 Walmarts.**
- **The honest cost, always:** Hebrew National won the general market and lost
  the glatt-kosher base. Thrivent broadened from ~6M Lutherans to ~200M
  Christians (72% vote, Apr 2013) and had to adopt a Financial Neutrality
  Policy that the LCMS objected to publicly. **Every successful case paid a
  price at the boundary. A plan that claims otherwise is not credible.**
- **Do not scrub the identity.** It is the only mechanic with a clean documented
  failure: Banco Popular renamed to "Popular Community Bank," then sold its
  Chicago and California operations within four years. Aunt Jemima →
  Pearl Milling: brand sales fell ~24% and had not recovered by end-2023; a
  peer-reviewed choice experiment found removing the *image* alone had no
  effect, while **changing the name cut willingness-to-pay 38.5%.**
- **And the payload from that same experiment: explaining why recovered most of
  the loss** — WTP loss fell from 38.5% to 2.0–5.6% when the change was
  narrated. Narrate every change.
- **Goya: the premise is backwards.** Boycott posts ran 75% higher than buycott
  posts, and **net sales rose ~22%** in the following two weeks. The mechanism
  is the useful part: canned beans (many substitutes) dipped in Democratic
  counties; **adobo seasoning (few substitutes) held steady everywhere.** The
  B2B translation is the argument to actually make — *the community-specific
  depth that makes Empeon the Orthodox operators' vendor is the adobo. Diluting
  it to look generic converts the company from adobo into beans.*

## The analytical spine, if it comes up

**Uzzi 1997, "The Paradox of Embeddedness"** (*ASQ* 42(1), ~9,600 citations):
embeddedness produces real economies "up to a threshold, however, after which
embeddedness can derail economic performance by making firms **vulnerable to
exogenous shocks** or **insulating them from information that exists beyond
their network**." Both liabilities materialized at Empeon in the same 18 months
— CDPAP is exactly an exogenous shock, and a referral-dense base returns almost
no signal about what national buyers want.

**Uzzi 1999** is the prescription: the highest-performing configuration is a
**mix of embedded and arm's-length ties**, because arm's-length ties are the
only source of market-price and market-requirement information. **The argument
is not "leave the base." It is "you currently have no arm's-length ties at all,
and that is a measurable performance cost, not just a growth ceiling."**

**Granovetter 1973:** strong ties recirculate what the group already has; novel
information arrives across weak ties. A referral-dense community is a strong-tie
network by construction and is therefore **structurally incapable** of carrying
the company into distant markets — not from hostility, but because strong ties
have no bridges.

**Portes & Shafer 2007** is the sharpest statement of the transfer problem: the
Mariel-era Cuban arrivals "benefited little from the pre-1980 enclave… **No
'character loans' were available to prospective new entrepreneurs for they were
not known to Cuban bank officials.**" Trust in these systems is *particular*,
not *categorical* — it did not extend even to co-ethnics who arrived later.

**HHAeXchange is the best precedent and it is in Empeon's own competitive set.**
Founded 2007 in Brooklyn at the request of a single home health agency. It did
not solve the trust-transfer problem — **it routed around it**, selling to
Medicaid managed care organizations and then to state Medicaid agencies as the
EVV aggregator, where credibility is *contractual* and awarded by RFP. Where it
needed agency relationships in a market where it had none, it **bought the
incumbent** (Cashé's 400 Minnesota agencies, then Sandata's 23 state EVV
contracts).

**And the pacing warning: Mindbody.** Two adjacent-segment acquisitions plus a
go-to-market reorganization in one calendar year broke a working sales motion.
CEO on the Nov 2018 call: *"we've been humbled by the last couple of quarters."*
Taken private four weeks later. **Empeon is currently moving on segment,
geography and product simultaneously with ~105 people.** Procore ran the same
three as *separately tracked axes* with a rebuilt sales org per stakeholder.
