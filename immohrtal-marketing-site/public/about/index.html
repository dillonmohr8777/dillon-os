The person behind the system

A hands on studio, not a mystery box

Dillon designs and builds websites, writes the pages people need, and connects the finished experience to search, HubSpot, and useful AI workers. The goal is not to add an AI label to ordinary agency work. The goal is to make the business easier to understand, trust, and contact.

The process starts with what a customer actually sees: what the business offers, why it is credible, what feels confusing, and what happens after someone reaches out. Design, content, search visibility, measurement, and follow-up are treated as one connected experience.

The operating thesis

A website is the first impression, the explanation, and the handoff

A polished homepage can earn attention, but attention alone does not make a site perform. The information needs a clear hierarchy. Key pages need crawlable HTML. Claims need support. Structured data needs to match what people can actually see. Forms and analytics need to deliver reliable context to the right place.

That is why IMMOHRTAL centers its work around three connected layers:

  • First impression

    Art direction, motion, responsive design, and accessibility that make the business feel specific and trustworthy.

  • Clear explanation

    Plain language, useful pages, supporting proof, and links that help people and search tools understand the business.

  • Clean handoff

    Forms, HubSpot routing, analytics, AI workers, and approval steps that move the right information to the right place.

  • Proof it works

    Browser checks, source records, mobile testing, and live release receipts instead of vague completion claims.

How the work moves

Check, choose, build, connect, prove

  1. Check what is happening now. Review the live site, the mobile experience, what customers can find, where they get stuck, and which tools receive their information.
  2. Choose one focused direction. Define who the site is for, what it needs them to understand, what proof matters, and what a useful result looks like.
  3. Build the public experience. Create memorable pages with clear language, strong structure, useful proof, and responsive behavior.
  4. Connect the handoff. Route forms, HubSpot context, analytics, research, and review steps without mixing client data or giving away unnecessary access.
  5. Prove it before calling it finished. Check mobile layouts, keyboard use, reduced motion, search access, links, builds, and the live website.

AI needs boundaries

Let AI handle the busywork, not the final say

AI workers can research, inspect websites, organize proof, draft content, check a build, and prepare the next step. Important actions still need the right approval. That includes sending outreach, publishing outside an approved website, changing ad spend, choosing an unclear account, or crossing a login challenge.

A useful worker should be able to explain what it checked, which client it served, what evidence it used, and where a person approved the work. If it cannot, it is not ready to act for a business.
