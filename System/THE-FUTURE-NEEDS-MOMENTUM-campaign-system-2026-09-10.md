# The future needs Momentum.

## Campaign strategy and copy system

Prepared for Dillon Mohr. September 10, 2026. Draft for creative review.

No images, videos, designs, public pages, campaigns, or paid generation were created or activated. This document replaces the earlier creative direction and volume target only. It does not approve commercial offers, client claims, budgets, or operational commitments.

## 1. The advertising idea

The future needs Momentum is the campaign signature. It is not the opening headline of every ad. Each execution should give a customer a different reason to care, then let the signature collect that meaning over time.

The underlying belief is that progress should make a good business more itself: easier to understand, easier to choose, more responsive, and less occupied by avoidable routine work. AI is a means of putting marketing judgment and business knowledge to work, not the whole story.

Proposed plain description:

Momentum AI brings marketing experience and practical AI together to help businesses get found, respond thoughtfully, and make more room for their best work.

This is positioning draft language, not a guarantee of commercial outcomes.

The commercial audience in the current internal positioning packet is established local service businesses in the Philadelphia region, initially within verified Momentum relationships. They already have a business, a website, demand, and a repeated problem worth addressing. They are not primarily buying an abstract vision of AI.

Their question is: what would actually be better about my business after working with you?

Our job is to answer that question in thirty different, specific ways. The answer must not always be a claim about speed or scale.

## 2. Classic craft, not a period costume

Borrow the discipline from classic advertising. Know the buyer. Find the product truth. Make one intelligible promise. Supply a reason to believe. Give the reader a useful next step. Test the result rather than assuming a clever headline must work.

The historical references are inspiration, not proof that a particular word count, font, or formula will guarantee performance. Ogilvy's archive demonstrates specific differentiation and story appeal. Hopkins's Scientific Advertising explicitly addresses specificity, useful service, and test campaigns.

Do not confuse this with dressing every scene in a Manhattan office, giving a fictional executive a whiskey, adding a gold atom, or writing on every object in the room. Those choices make the agency the subject instead of the customer's life.

Keep the confidence, editorial craft, wit, and deliberate restraint. Make the human situations contemporary and relevant.

Your marketing education, Mac's Google experience, and Sean's sales experience can support the proof layer after the precise bios and permissions are verified. They should help explain the team's judgment, not turn every ad into a resume.

## 3. Message architecture

Permanent signature: The future needs Momentum.

Permanent identity: the actual Momentum mark, a separate AI descriptor where approved, the approved Momentum blue, coherent typography, and a warm, observant voice.

Variable elements: customer situation, headline, promise, mechanism, evidence, narrative rhythm, setting, and call to action.

An ad needs one customer situation, one main benefit, one reason to believe, and one action. It does not need every service, every credential, and every slogan at once.

AI need not appear in every headline. The body should explain where it has a real role. Do not conceal automation in a service description, but do not make a model name substitute for the customer benefit.

The campaign is six related families: human judgment, time and useful work, customer demand, search and discovery, creative and recognition, and practical progress. These are planning categories, not additional public slogans.

## 4. Voice rules

Write the way a perceptive person would explain the idea to a business owner. Use ordinary details, useful questions, understatement, and occasional wit. Vary sentence length. Let the point land before adding another one.

Use customer, inquiry, reply, Friday, question, owner, conversation, and next step where those words are more accurate than ecosystem, orchestration, transformation, or scale.

Retire the prior three word contrast slogan. Avoid reflexive chains of fragments. Do not replace every ordinary verb with accelerate, amplify, elevate, or unlock. A line can be quiet and still be persuasive.

No dashes in advertising copy. Do not force a seven or eight word count. Write the strongest natural sentence, then decide how it should break across the frame.

The thirty records below are draft advertising, not verified outcome claims. Each includes a proof requirement and a proposed CTA. Before public use, confirm that the offer and destination exist and that the claim is supportable. Keep prices, delivery dates, numerical savings, client names, guaranteed rankings, and guaranteed revenue out of this draft system.

## 7. Typography with different voices and one identity

### Primary editorial voice: Domaine Display

Recommend Domaine Display Medium or Semibold for human, thoughtful headlines. The foundry describes a contemporary serif drawing on traditional French and British forms. Our use would favor sentence case, deliberate line breaks, and enough space for the curves to be visible. Italic is emphasis, not decoration on every line.

### Shared reading voice: Söhne

Recommend Söhne Buch for body text and Söhne Kräftig or Halbfett for labels, captions, practical headlines, and actions. Keep this reading family constant across films, stills, decks, and explanatory pages. It also gives the more practical ads a confident voice without using the same oversized condensed treatment everywhere.

### Occasional witty voice: Ohno Fatface

Recommend Ohno Fatface for selected messages with a little humor, such as the repeated copying line or the better Tuesday idea. It is an expressive option, not the whole identity. Pair it with Söhne rather than combining it with Domaine in the same frame.

These are contemporary typeface recommendations with classic sensibilities, not a claim of exact 1950s authenticity. Maximum two families per execution. No unlicensed production use, assumed ownership, font redistribution, or purchase in this planning phase.

### Spacing is part of the writing

For a 1920 by 1080 film, start exploration with about 120 pixels of horizontal inset and 96 pixels vertically. Explore headline sizes around 110 to 144 pixels and supporting text around 38 to 46 pixels. For a 1080 by 1350 still, start with 80 pixel margins, headlines around 76 to 104 pixels, and supporting copy around 30 to 36 pixels.

These are starting design decisions, not universal platform safe areas. Recompose each format. Check the actual delivery interface and phone size before approval. Do not crop a landscape poster and call it a vertical design.

Headline line spacing should generally begin around 1.02 to 1.12 times the type size; body text around 1.25 to 1.4. Adjust for the specific face and wording. Avoid exaggerated letter spacing on sentences. Reserve uppercase for short utility labels. Give the CTA its own breathing room rather than attaching it to the paragraph.

A natural first line break:

The tools changed.
People are still people.

A different natural rhythm:

Your best people have
better things to do.

A headline is a thought, not a stack of isolated words. Do not shrink the type to fit an overcrowded composition. Edit the frame or the copy. Leave a substantial quiet area and one obvious starting point for the eye. Not every corner needs text.

The default palette is the verified Momentum blue, warm white, and a dark neutral. New supporting colors would be a separate approved campaign choice. Do not turn the blue logo gold, emboss it into props, or invent a decorative brand variant.

## 9. A useful conversion path

The first action should match the ad's promise. A search readiness ad should lead to an approved search review or Snapshot route. A workflow ad should lead to a conversation about one repeated process. A creative ad should lead to a discussion of one campaign. A prototype ad should explain the prototype boundary.

Do not promise a free audit, immediate booking, or a specified turnaround simply because a CTA needs somewhere to go. Snapshot availability and delivery are proposed in the source plans, not verified as live by this campaign brief. Validate destinations and form handling before public production.

A later landing page should preserve the ad headline's meaning, show one concrete example, explain what happens after the inquiry, and make only the claims supported by the relevant offer. No generic homepage destination for every message by default.

## 10. A testing plan, not invented winners

No concept in this document is a measured winner. The first five are a reasoned creative shortlist.

First, use a small qualitative review to ask what the viewer thinks Momentum offers, who it is for, and what they expect after clicking. Do not treat five friendly reactions as a statistically valid market test. Resolve confusion before spending.

When a paid test is separately approved, compare message concepts using the same relevant audience, objective, destination quality, and comparable delivery conditions. Pick the success criterion and spending limit beforehand. Once a message shows useful signal, test a headline or presentation change without changing everything else at once.

Track concept ID through impression, click, inquiry, qualification, sales conversation, proposal, and accepted work where permissioned measurement supports it. Distinguish these stages. Record unmatched cases rather than manufacturing attribution. Use attention metrics as diagnostic evidence, not the sole definition of success.

Do not split a small budget across all thirty concepts. Begin with a manageable subset tied to a commercially ready offer. A campaign bank is not a daily production quota.
