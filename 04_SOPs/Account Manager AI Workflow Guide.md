## Every major point from the recording

Transcribed from the walkthrough, grouped by theme. Timestamps refer to the 9:18 edited cut.

### Why the video exists — 00:00

- The audience is account managers and the wider organization.
- The goal is a **repeatable daily AI workflow**, not a tool demo.
- The intended outcome is that routine work gets easier and more consistent.

### Delegating operational friction — 00:10

- Dillon had never recorded a Loom before, so he asked Codex to download and set it up.
- The monitor had no webcam available, so he asked it to handle the recording and connect
  to his Bluetooth headphones for the mic.
- **The point:** knowing how to ask for operational help removes friction from everyday work.
  The recording itself is the example.

### ChatGPT and Codex have converged — 00:51

- The frontier model that used to live only in the coding app is now in the ChatGPT app.
- The integration ecosystem is the main reason the tools are more useful now.
- **ChatGPT Work is where most account managers should spend their time.**
- Codex remains the better surface for coding, longer workflows, and web design.
- Non-technical users can now benefit from capabilities that previously felt coding-specific.

### The recommended starting setup — 01:30

1. **Download the ChatGPT desktop app** — not just a browser tab. The desktop app is what
   makes plugins, browser control, and computer use usable.
2. **Tell it your role and your day-to-day responsibilities.** Let it get to know you.
3. **Ask it directly what you should integrate with** for that role.
4. **Use Plan Mode.** Choosing a formal project is optional for most ordinary tasks.
- Workflows should be divided by role, personalized, and still simple enough to actually adopt.

### Plans, models, and cost — 02:46

- Dillon is on the $200/month plan because of client volume, organizational work, complex
  workflows, and freelance work. **Most of the team does not need that.**
- **A $20 plan gets you roughly 80% of the top-tier capability.**
- Run the mid-tier model on **medium** reasoning for daily work; step up to **high** or
  **extra high** only when a task genuinely needs the depth.
- The most expensive option is not automatically the better performer for this kind of work.
- Spend the difference on connecting your stack properly instead.

### Plan Mode and daily-driver value — 03:49

- Plan Mode is used to personalize role-based workflows; take the plan it recommends as the
  starting point.
- The models are getting genuinely capable — this is a daily driver, not a novelty.
- The payoff is better client communication and the ability to do more, effectively.

### Client-facing safeguards — 04:17

- It **will** send emails and updates for you. **Personalize your voice, and always
  double-check before sending — especially anything client-facing.**
- Personalization matters more than raw automation.
- Small wins compound: an embedded, role-personalized **HTML email signature** took minutes
  and landed well with another team member.
- The organizational goal is to step our game up wherever we can.

### The automation claim — 04:59

- **One prompt can expedite or automate 50–60% of what most account managers do.**
- The intended result is **time given back**, not just more output.

### Plugins and context — 05:16

- **Push the AI and make it know you. Context is the multiplier.**
- A generic assistant is useful; one that knows your clients, your cadence, and your voice
  is a different tool entirely.
- **Gmail is the first integration to set up.**

### Figma, Chrome, and browser control — 05:38

- Figma is a strong plugin where design work is involved.
- Chrome lets it control your browser; it is very good at computer use and browser use.
- Dillon deliberately exited a screen mid-recording rather than risk exposing organizational
  or client information — **convenience does not override privacy judgment.**

### Documentation and handoff — 05:51

- Documents and a transcript with bullet points and exact instructions would follow, so
  nobody has to watch the whole video — **but you do have to download the plugins.**
- Codex can act as a lightweight personal task system; you could use it as your Asana.
- Dedicated integrations like Dropbox still matter where they are the approved system.

### Role-specific tools — 06:23

- **Canva** for anyone doing social media.
- **Developer tools** for anyone working on the backend.
- Codex is Dillon's preferred mainstream daily driver — it understands him better than the
  alternatives.
- **Claude** is used constantly for video editing and web design; several models are strong
  at web design.
- Your workflow mix should match your actual responsibilities, not copy anyone else's stack.

### Slack and Gmail as organizational memory — 07:05

- **Give it access to Slack and Gmail** where that is appropriate for your role.
- The more you use it, the more it helps. It becomes a daily driver.
- Open a new chat and **recommendations can already be waiting** based on your work context —
  click one, hit enter, done.

### Personal use — 07:20

- Apple Music is connected for discovering new artists and music. AI is useful in personal
  life too; that could be a separate video.
- Keep personal integrations separate from client workflows and client data.

### CRM and Google integrations — 07:55

- **HubSpot** is really good and a great plugin if your role uses HubSpot.
- **Google Sheets** and the other Google integrations are broadly useful.

### Advertising integrations — 08:04

- An **Ads Manager for ChatGPT** is emerging — worth keeping an eye on.
- **LinkedIn Ads** if you run LinkedIn campaigns; **Google Ads** and **Google Shopping**
  integrations are also available.
- A lot of the team runs ads, but not every advertising integration is relevant to every
  role — keep setup role-specific.

### Keyword research and marketing tools — 08:35

- **Semrush** is a strong option for keyword research.
- **Google Keyword Planner** is also very good.
- The plugin marketplace is worth exploring — there are hundreds of plugins, including new
  ones that were new to Dillon during the recording.

### Security and staying in control — 09:01

- **Be careful. Do not let sensitive client information be exposed.**
- You can give it browser access, but **you remain the person delegating the work.** Stay in
  control.
- With that oversight in place, you are good.

| 04 | Repeatable process | Three copy-paste prompts and the four non-negotiables |

### The five-stage loop

Every account manager runs the same five stages in the same order. Only the connected
accounts and role modules change.

1. **Collect** — pull today's real context from email, Slack, CRM, and client files.
2. **Prioritize** — turn it into urgent actions, due dates, blockers, open client decisions.
3. **Produce** — draft the update, report, brief, or analysis.
4. **Review** — check the client, dates, metrics, tone, and recipients. Every time.
5. **Deliver** — a human approves anything consequential: sending, publishing, spending.

### Tier 1 — the core six

Gmail · Slack · Google Sheets & Drive · Google Calendar · HubSpot · Chrome

### Tier 2 — add by role

| Module | Plugins |
|---|---|
| Paid Media | Google Ads, Google Shopping, LinkedIn Ads, Google Analytics |
| Analytics & SEO | Semrush, Google Keyword Planner, Google Analytics, Sheets |
| Content & Social | Canva, Figma, WordPress |
| Web & Technical | Codex, Claude, GitHub |
| Files & Projects | Dropbox, Asana, Notion |
| Recording & Training | Loom, transcription and caption tooling, automation layer |

### The four non-negotiables

1. Double-check every client-facing send.
2. Never expose sensitive client information.
3. You stay the person delegating.
4. Verify the account, date range, and conversion definition before you report.

## Regenerating the PDF

The build is fully scripted so the guide can be updated without rebuilding the design.

```bash
cd 04_SOPs/assets/am-ai-workflow
python3 build.py ../../"Account Manager AI Workflow Guide.pdf"
```

**What the build needs**

- **Fonts** — Montserrat and Lato on the font path. Run `./fetch-fonts.sh` once to install
  them (both are SIL Open Font License, fetched rather than vendored to keep the vault lean).
- **Chromium** — the script shells out to Chromium's `--print-to-pdf`. Update the binary
  path at the bottom of `build.py` if yours differs.
- **Assets** — `assets/` holds the official Momentum 360 lockup plus the derived logo mark
  and cover watermark. `icons/paths.json` holds the plugin icon paths and each vendor's
  official brand color, so every plugin renders in its own real logo.
