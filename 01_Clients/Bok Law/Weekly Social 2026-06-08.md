## Wednesday Wisdom
**Post date:** Wednesday, June 11, 2026

### Hook
Summer's almost here. So are the custody schedule questions that keep both parents up at night.

### Body
When school lets out, a parenting plan that worked fine in September can suddenly feel like a puzzle with missing pieces. Who gets the kids for the Fourth of July? Can one parent take a week at the beach? What happens when Grandma's birthday falls on your scheduled weekend?

These aren't small details. They're the moments your kids will remember.

A few things that help families stay steady through summer:

• **Start with the plan you already have.** Most custody orders include summer provisions. Read them before the first pool day, not after a conflict starts.

• **Talk early, not in the parking lot.** Bring up vacation dates and special events weeks ahead. A quick calendar review in May saves a lot of stress in July.

• **Put it in writing when plans change.** A friendly text agreement is fine for small swaps. Bigger schedule shifts deserve a written record both parents can reference.

• **Keep the kids out of the middle.** They shouldn't be messengers, negotiators, or judges of who's being fair.

Summer should feel like freedom for your children, not a season of tension between households. When parents plan ahead and communicate with respect, kids get to focus on popsicles and sleepovers instead of wondering which house they'll be at next week.

### CTA
If your summer schedule feels unclear, mediation can help both parents map out a plan before the season gets busy. Reach out when you're ready to talk it through.

## Turn the Page Thursday
**Post date:** Thursday, June 12, 2026

### Hook
The last school bell rings. For a lot of families, that's not just summer starting. That's a whole new chapter.

### Body
June brings graduations, end-of-year concerts, and the quiet shift from one routine to another. Maybe your youngest just finished elementary school. Maybe a teenager is heading to college in the fall. Maybe you're a parent navigating co-parenting through a season that looks different than it did last year.

Every ending carries a little grief and a lot of possibility.

You don't have to have the next chapter figured out today. You just have to be willing to turn the page.

• Honor what you're leaving behind. The years mattered.

• Give yourself permission to feel mixed emotions. Relief and sadness can sit in the same room.

• Look for one small thing you're excited about on the other side of this transition.

Life doesn't move in straight lines. Neither do families. A new season doesn't erase what came before. It just gives you fresh ground to stand on.

### CTA
Whatever chapter you're stepping into, you don't have to write it alone. We're here when you need a steady voice in the room.

### Suggested image direction
Open book with a turning page, soft golden-hour light. Subtle graduation cap or summer flowers in the background. Hopeful, warm palette. Clean and uncluttered.

## Family Fridays
**Post date:** Friday, June 13, 2026

### Hook
Pittsburgh summers don't need a big budget. They just need a little time and someone to share them with.

### Body
There's something about early June in the Burgh that makes you want to slow down. The days stretch longer. The farmers markets are back. Kids are counting down to that last day of school.

Some of our favorite low-key family moments don't cost a thing:

• A walk through Schenley Park while the fireflies start showing up at dusk

• Picking up fresh strawberries at a neighborhood market and eating half of them in the car

• Letting the kids run through a sprinkler in the backyard while you sit in a lawn chair and actually finish a conversation

• Catching a free outdoor concert or movie night somewhere in the city

Strong families aren't built on perfect vacations. They're built on ordinary Fridays that feel like enough.

Here's to a summer full of the simple stuff.

### CTA
Tag someone you'd want on your next Pittsburgh summer adventure. Or tell us your favorite low-cost family tradition in the comments.

Candid family moment at a Pittsburgh farmers market or park. Bright, natural light. Laughing kids, fresh produce, casual summer clothes. Community vibe, not staged stock photo energy.
