### Wednesday Wisdom: June 18, 2026
**Topic:** Summer custody schedules and co-parenting transitions

When school lets out, the calendar that worked all year often doesn't. Pickup times shift. Camps fill whole weeks. One parent may have more daytime hours than they're used to.

If you're sharing custody, summer can feel like starting over every Monday. That's normal. Kids need predictability even when the schedule changes. A few habits help:

• Share the summer calendar early. Include camps, travel, and any blackout dates before conflicts turn into last-minute arguments.
• Keep handoffs consistent. Same location, same time when you can. Familiar routines lower anxiety for everyone.
• Talk to your kids about the plan in plain language. They don't need every detail, but they do need to know where they'll sleep tonight.
• Build in a little grace. Weather, traffic, and tired kids happen. A flexible minute today can prevent a fight tomorrow.

Summer isn't a break from co-parenting. It's a different season that needs the same respect, communication, and patience you've been practicing all year.

### Turn the Page Thursday: June 19, 2026
**Topic:** Closing one chapter without closing the door on yourself

Some endings arrive quietly. A signed agreement. A move to a new apartment. A child's last day in a school they've outgrown. You wake up and realize the life you were living yesterday isn't the one you're in today.

That shift can feel heavy and hopeful at the same time. You're allowed to grieve what didn't work while still reaching for what might.

Turning the page doesn't mean pretending the hard parts didn't happen. It means you stop rereading the same painful paragraph every morning. You notice what's still yours: your health, your friendships, your ability to learn, your next small decision.

You don't have to have the whole next chapter mapped out. One page at a time is enough. Write something kind to yourself on this one.

### Family Fridays: June 20, 2026
**Topic:** Pittsburgh summer nights with the people who matter

Long daylight, open sidewalks, and that first warm evening when nobody wants to go inside yet. Pittsburgh summers have a way of pulling families back together.

Maybe it's a Pirates game where the kids care more about the pierogi race than the score. Maybe it's a walk through the Strip District for a shared pretzel and too many samples. Maybe it's just sitting on the porch while the neighborhood hums back to life.

You don't need a perfect plan or a big budget. You need presence. Put the phone down for twenty minutes. Ask a question you haven't asked in a while. Let someone pick the playlist.

Here's to the small Friday moments that become the stories you'll tell later. Pittsburgh's good at those. So are you.
