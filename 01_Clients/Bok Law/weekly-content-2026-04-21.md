# BOK Law Weekly Content — Week of April 21

## Wednesday Wisdom (April 23)

**When Your Kids Ask About the Divorce**

One of the hardest moments in any separation isn't the paperwork. It isn't the court date. It's the conversation at the kitchen table when your child looks up and asks what's happening.

There's no script for that moment. Every family is different. Every child processes change differently. A four-year-old needs simpler words than a twelve-year-old. A teenager might need more space than answers.

What most family law professionals agree on: honesty calibrated to their age, reassurance that both parents love them, and consistency in the days that follow. Children don't need every detail. They need to feel safe.

If you're approaching this conversation and aren't sure where to start, a family therapist can help you prepare. Many mediators also offer co-parenting communication frameworks that give both parents a shared language to use.

You don't have to get it perfect. You just have to show up with intention.

## Turn the Page Thursday (April 24)

**The First Morning That Feels Normal**

It doesn't arrive on a schedule. You can't plan for it. One morning you wake up, make coffee, and realize the weight is a little lighter than yesterday.

Maybe it's the first time you laughed without thinking about it. Maybe it's the first Saturday morning that felt like yours again. Maybe it's as small as choosing a new route to work and feeling good about it.

Healing doesn't announce itself. It just shows up in the ordinary moments. And when it does, you're allowed to notice it without guilt.

New chapters don't start with fireworks. They start with quiet mornings and small choices.

## Family Fridays (April 25)

**Pittsburgh Spring: The Season of Reconnection**

Pittsburgh in late April has a specific energy. The trails along the Three Rivers are filling back up. Schenley Park picnic tables are first-come, first-served again. The farmers markets are opening across the city.

If you're looking for a low-key weekend with your family, here are a few ideas:

- Walk the Eliza Furnace Trail from the South Side to Oakland. Flat, easy, and the river views reset your whole week.
- Hit up the Bloomfield Saturday Market for fresh bread and people-watching.
- Take the Duquesne Incline at sunset. It never gets old, even if you've lived here your whole life.

Spring in Pittsburgh is a reminder that things come back. Even when winter felt long.
