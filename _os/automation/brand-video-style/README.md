## The rules these encode

- **Handmade paper is the substrate.** Deckle edges, visible fibre, real
  thickness. Never flat vector, never a paper *filter* over digital shapes.
- **One indigo accent per frame.** Exactly one. It carries the eye and marks
  the signal. Everything else is cream, kraft, bone, brass, graphite.
- **Real light.** Soft raking daylight, honest contact shadow, shallow depth of
  field on macro. Objects sit on a surface and cast onto it.
- **Physical hardware, not icons.** Brass brads, paperclips, washi and glassine
  tape, thread, scalpel, bone folder.
- **Construction lines stay visible.** Pencil axes, registration marks, tape.
  The making is part of the image.
- **Engraving for detail, papercraft for form.** Fine hatched line work reads
  as the "technical" layer; torn and folded paper reads as the "built" layer.

## Motion, when these become video

Paper-craft stop-motion weight. Things have mass — they settle, they do not
bounce. No rubber easing, no digital swoosh, no glow. A held frame is allowed
to be still.
