# Website Fixes & Report Card Playbook — Conversion and Plain-English Reporting

## Website Fixes — finding what costs you leads

The leak audit, in priority order (fix top-down; each item above typically outweighs everything below it):
1. **Speed**: mobile load over 3 seconds bleeds visitors before they see anything. Compress images, lazy-load, drop unused scripts/plugins.
2. **Above-the-fold clarity**: within 5 seconds a visitor must know what you do, where, and how to act. Headline = service + area + outcome. Phone number visible, click-to-call on mobile.
3. **One primary CTA repeated**: call or book — pick one, make it a colored button, repeat it every screen-height. Competing CTAs split conversion.
4. **Message match**: the page a visitor lands on must repeat the promise of the ad/result they clicked. Mismatch = instant bounce.
5. **Trust close to the CTA**: review stars, count, guarantee, badges — within one screen of every CTA, not quarantined on an About page.
6. **Forms**: 3–4 fields maximum (name, phone, what do you need). Every extra field cuts completion. Confirm what happens next after submit.
7. **Mobile reality check**: 60–80% of local service traffic is mobile. Test the actual phone experience: tap targets, sticky call button, no interstitials.
8. **Tracking**: call tracking + form tracking + GA4 events. You can't fix what you can't attribute.

Deliverable format: a prioritized leak list — each item = the leak, the evidence, the fix, and the estimated lead impact — in plain English, ordered by revenue impact.

## Report Card — plain-English reporting

The reporting philosophy: an owner should read the report in 3 minutes and know exactly three things — what happened, why, and what we're doing next. No dashboards required.

**The monthly report card structure**:
1. **Headline** (one sentence): "You got 47 qualified leads this month, up 18% — here's what drove it."
2. **Leads & revenue first**: calls, forms, booked jobs, cost per lead by channel. Rankings and traffic are supporting evidence, never the lead story.
3. **What worked / what didn't**: 2–3 bullets each, honest. Trust compounds when misses are reported as plainly as wins.
4. **Next month's moves**: 3 specific actions with expected impact.
5. **The asks**: anything needed from the owner (photos, review responses, budget decision).

**Attribution rules**: every lead gets a source (call tracking numbers per channel, UTM-tagged forms, "how did you hear about us" as backstop). When attribution is fuzzy, say so — an honest "mixed" beats a confident lie.

**Grading**: give each channel a letter grade against its target (A = beat target, C = at target, F = investigate). Owners think in grades; it forces the agency to commit to targets.
