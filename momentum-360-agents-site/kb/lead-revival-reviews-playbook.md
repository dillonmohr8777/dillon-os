# Lead Revival & Review Management Playbook

## Lead Reviver — waking up cold leads

Most businesses sit on a goldmine: quotes that never closed, form fills never called twice, past customers gone quiet. Reactivation is the cheapest revenue available — the list already knows you.

**The 9-word email** (highest response rate in the playbook): subject is the person's name or blank-feeling, body is a single question — "Are you still looking to get your roof replaced?" No signature block, no images, no links. It reads personal because it is.

**The revival sequence** (email + SMS where consented):
1. Day 0 — 9-word email.
2. Day 3 — value touch: a genuinely useful tip related to their original request.
3. Day 7 — offer touch: small incentive with a real deadline ("we have 2 install slots left this month").
4. Day 14 — breakup email: "Should I close your file?" — this one gets replies.
Stop the sequence the moment they reply. Route replies to a human within minutes.

**Past-customer reactivation**: seasonal service reminders (tune-ups, inspections, maintenance), anniversary check-ins ("your water heater turns 8 this year"), and referral asks 2 weeks after a 5-star review.

**List hygiene**: only contact people who gave you their info; honor opt-outs instantly; SMS requires prior express consent (TCPA) — when in doubt, email only.

## Review Manager — reputation that ranks and converts

Reviews do double duty: they convert prospects AND move map-pack rankings (count, velocity, recency, and text content all matter).

**Getting reviews**:
- Ask at the peak-happiness moment: job completed, problem visibly solved. In person beats email; a text with a direct GBP link beats both for completion rate.
- Make it a process, not a favor: every completed job triggers the ask. Target steady velocity (a few per week) over bursts — bursts look bought.
- Never gate ("only ask happy customers via a filter survey") — it violates Google policy — and never buy or incentivize reviews.

**Responding (respond to 100%, within 48h)**:
- 5-star: thank by name, mention the specific job ("glad the new opener install went smoothly") — this feeds keywords into your profile naturally.
- 3–4 star: thank, own the gap, state the fix.
- 1–2 star: stay calm, never argue details publicly, apologize for the experience (not necessarily fault), move it offline ("call me directly at..."), then actually resolve it. Prospects read your worst-review response more carefully than your best review.
- Fake/wrong-business reviews: flag with Google, respond neutrally noting no record of the customer while it's reviewed.

**Tone template**: sound like the owner on a good day — warm, specific, brief. Never copy-paste the same response text; Google and readers both notice.
