## Wednesday Wisdom — August 6, 2026

**Topic:** School-year custody schedules and what to document early

**Caption:**

The first week of August is when a lot of Pittsburgh families start thinking about school calendars, pickup windows, and who handles what when the routine changes.

If you share custody, small gaps in the plan become big stress points once classes start. One parent books a fall activity without checking the schedule. A bus route change shifts pickup by twenty minutes. A teacher conference lands on a day that wasn't spelled out in the parenting plan.

What helps most is writing down the practical details before they're urgent:

• **Exchange times and locations** for school days vs weekends vs holidays
• **Who registers** for school, activities, and medical appointments
• **How schedule changes get communicated** (direct between parents, not through the kids)
• **What happens when work schedules shift** mid-semester

You don't need a courtroom fight to fix a calendar problem. Often a clear email trail or a short addendum to your parenting plan is enough to keep August planning from turning into September conflict.

## Turn the Page Thursday — August 7, 2026

**Topic:** Choosing clarity over conflict in mediation

**Caption:**

Mediation isn't about winning. It's about building a plan you can actually live with.

We've seen couples walk into mediation convinced the other person won't budge. By the second session, they're often surprised at how much they agree on when the conversation is structured and the focus stays on outcomes instead of history.

What shifts the room:

• **Preparing your priorities** before you sit down (what matters most for the kids, the house, the timeline)
• **Listening for interests**, not just positions ("I need stability" vs "I want the house")
• **Separating financial decisions from emotional ones** — they rarely belong in the same conversation
• **Giving yourself permission to pause** when a topic needs more thought

Turning the page doesn't mean forgetting what happened. It means deciding what comes next is worth more than replaying what didn't work.

## Family Fridays — August 8, 2026

**Topic:** Pittsburgh summer winding down — family moments that matter

**Caption:**

August in Pittsburgh means one last push at the pool, a few more evenings on the deck, and the quiet shift when kids start asking if school starts "next week or the week after."

For families navigating change, those small end-of-summer rituals still matter. A last trip to Kennywood. A backyard cookout with cousins. A slow morning with no agenda.

You don't have to manufacture perfect moments. Sometimes the win is a normal Friday where everyone ate dinner, nobody fought over screens, and someone laughed at something silly.

If this summer brought hard conversations or big transitions, give yourself credit for the ordinary days that held steady. Those count too.
