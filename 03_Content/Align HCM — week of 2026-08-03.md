## Monday, August 4 — Thought Leadership (Maher El-Abdallah)

**Format:** Native text post (optional 60–90 sec talking-head video)

**Post copy:**

The question I hear most after an HCM go-live isn't "Did we launch?" It's "Why does my team still open tickets for things we thought we fixed in testing?"

Usually the answer isn't the platform. It's ownership.

Go-live transfers the system. It doesn't automatically transfer the judgment for how time rules should work, how approvals should route, or who owns configuration when the business changes.

Teams that stabilize faster do three things:

• They name a **business owner** for HCM decisions, not just an IT contact for tickets.
• They schedule **monthly configuration reviews** in the first year, not annual fire drills.
• They treat **manager training** as ongoing, because the managers who struggle in month two are often the same ones who never got a real workflow walkthrough.

If your HCM still feels like a project six months in, look at ownership and rhythm before you look at software.

What's still falling through on your team after go-live? I'd love to hear what's actually happening in the field.

## Friday, August 8 — Thought Leadership (Joann)

**Format:** Native text post or carousel

**Post copy:**

Payroll accuracy isn't just a numbers problem. It's a trust problem.

When an employee's paycheck is wrong once, they forgive. When it happens twice, they start checking every line. When it becomes a pattern, they stop trusting the system — and sometimes they stop trusting leadership.

The fixes that restore trust are usually simpler than people expect:

• **Audit the inputs** before you blame the platform (time entries, rate changes, deduction updates).
• **Close the loop fast** when someone flags an issue — same-day acknowledgment beats a perfect answer next week.
• **Document what changed** so the same mistake doesn't repeat on the next cycle.

Payroll teams carry quiet pressure. A little operational discipline on the front end saves a lot of relationship repair on the back end.

What's the one payroll surprise your team is still trying to prevent? Always learning from what's happening in the field.
