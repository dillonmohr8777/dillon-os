## Monday, July 28 — Thought Leadership (Maher El-Abdallah)

**Format:** Native text post (optional 60–90 sec talking-head video)

**Post copy:**

Most HCM projects don't fail at go-live. They fail six months later, when the implementation partner has moved on and nobody owns what comes next.

I've watched strong teams lose ground on reporting, timekeeping rules, and manager self-service because they treated go-live as the finish line. It isn't. It's the starting line for actually running the business on the platform.

The organizations that get lasting ROI do three things differently:

• They plan for post-go-live support before the project kicks off, not after tickets start piling up.
• They assign internal ownership for configuration decisions, not just IT tickets.
• They treat optimization as an ongoing discipline, not a one-time cleanup sprint.

If your HCM platform still feels like a project instead of a system your team trusts, the gap is usually support and ownership, not the software itself.

## Friday, August 1 — Thought Leadership (Barbara Tonelli)

**Format:** Carousel (4–5 slides) or native text post

**Post copy:**

HR teams are being asked to do more with the same headcount. That's not a motivation problem. It's a systems problem.

When your HCM platform is configured for how the business actually runs, HR stops being the department that chases exceptions. It becomes the team that designs how work gets done.

Three signs your platform is working against you:

• Managers still email HR for things they should handle in self-service.
• Payroll corrections spike every cycle because time rules weren't built for real-world scenarios.
• Reporting takes days because nobody trusts the data enough to pull it without a manual audit.

Fixing this doesn't require a rip-and-replace. It requires someone who understands both the platform and how your operations actually work.

We've helped mid-market teams close these gaps without adding FTEs. The pattern is consistent: stabilize the basics, then optimize what managers and employees touch every day.

If your HR team is still the help desk for your HCM system, something in the configuration or support model needs to change.
