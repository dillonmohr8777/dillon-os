# Handoff — launch films, upgrade pass

**Summary:** Dillon handed this to Codex mid-upgrade. Everything below was verified
by running it, not by reasoning about it. Read this before touching the films so the
same walls do not get hit twice.

## Where it stands

Six films exist and render clean (`01`–`06` in this folder, PR #376). Dillon's
verdict on them: *"There's a lot of fonts that are cutting into each other"*,
*"some of it really suck"*, *"we need way better fonts"*. So the six are a working
skeleton, not a finished set. An upgrade pass was underway and was stopped partway.

Three things were finished and are safe to build on. One thing was rejected on
sight and needs redoing.

## 1. The font problem is solved — use these

`higgsedit` vendors **any Google Font**, not the two that were being used. Verified
installing: Instrument Serif, Sora, Geist, Space Grotesk, Manrope, Bricolage
Grotesque, Fraunces, Newsreader, DM Sans, Outfit, Familjen Grotesk, Chivo, Anton,
Syne, Unbounded, Gabarito. Fontshare-only faces (General Sans, Satoshi) are **not**
available.

The pairing that was rendered and looked right, replacing Archivo Black + Nunito
Sans:

```bash
higgsedit fonts add proj "Bricolage Grotesque:800" "Geist:500" "Geist:600"
```

- **Bricolage Grotesque 800** — display. At 126–150px with `letterSpacing={-4}` to
  `{-5}` it has real character and holds up over footage.
- **Geist 500/600** — UI, body, and eyebrows. At 24–30px, and at 26px with
  `letterSpacing={8}` for all-caps eyebrows.

A full judged type system was in flight when this stopped; it did not land. The
pairing above is proven by render, so it is the safe default.

## 2. The collisions were a layout bug, not a font bug — and it is fixed

Every overlap came from stacking text with hand-picked absolute `y` values. When a
string ran longer than guessed, it wrapped and ran into the line below.

**`<column gap={n}>` auto-flows and wraps.** Verified: a deliberately over-long line
was allowed to wrap to four lines and it *pushed the next line down* rather than
overlapping it. Also verified on columns: `padding` + `fill` + `radius` gives a real
card, `<row>` nests inside with its own gap, and `align="center"` works.

**Rule for the rebuild: never stack text with absolute `y` again. One column per
region.** Two columns at fixed `y` can still collide with *each other*, so give each
column its own band of the frame.

## 3. Iterate on frames, not renders

```bash
higgsedit frame proj 1.2 --out renders/a.png
```

About two seconds, versus a minute for a full film. Layout work should never wait on
a render.

## 4. The globe was rejected — this is where it was left

Dillon on the stylised dotted sphere: *"that world is fucking terrible. Like, I want
a realistic world... as realistic, but, like, still professional, animated."*

So the vector wireframe globe in film 4 **and** the stylised dot-sphere plate are
both out. Two photoreal replacements were started:

**(a) Higgsfield stills — done, and they look right.** Two ISS-perspective frames of
the US eastern seaboard at night, curved blue atmospheric limb, city lights. Job ids
`f04b7622-863b-4bc0-af85-6d8e317845bd` and `6039cd81-ea07-4a1b-9938-b8e97d40c4c7`.
The first is the stronger frame. **Next step was to animate one with Kling 3.0** —
slow orbital drift, locked exposure, no zoom — which was not run. Prompt that was
queued up:

> Slow orbital drift as seen from the International Space Station: the camera tracks
> slowly right along the terminator, city lights twinkling faintly, thin cloud banks
> drifting, the blue atmospheric limb holding steady. No cuts, no zoom, no text, no
> new objects. Photoreal, cinematic, locked exposure.

**(b) A real-satellite-data globe renderer — written, not finished.** Projects NASA
public-domain imagery onto a sphere, so it is genuinely photographic rather than an
illustration of a planet. The sandbox recycled before it finished, so the frames are
gone, but it is fully reproducible. Sources (all fetched successfully):

```
https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/79000/79765/dnb_land_ocean_ice.2012.3600x1800.jpg   # VIIRS night lights
https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/73000/73909/world.topo.bathy.200412.3x5400x2700.jpg # Blue Marble day
https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/144000/144898/BlackMarble_2016_01deg.jpg
https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/57000/57752/land_shallow_topo_2048.jpg
```

The renderer is saved beside this note as `globe-nasa.py`. It does an orthographic
projection with a real terminator, day/night blend, city-light bloom, limb darkening
and a blue atmospheric shell, and writes an RGBA frame sequence. At 1000px square it
ran at roughly one frame per second, so 240 frames (8s at 30fps) needs about four
minutes — run it with `background:true` and poll.

Route (a) is the faster path to something Dillon will approve. Route (b) is the one
that is literally real.

## 5. The look that was approved in principle

`scratchpad/pool/looktest.png` (sent to Dillon) proved the target: Bricolage
Grotesque 800 at 150px over a graded Philadelphia fireworks plate with a bottom
scrim, and the same treatment beside a globe. Two fixes noted at the time and not yet
applied: the scrim should start higher (`y=340`, not `420`) so the headline never sits
on the bridge, and the gold eyebrow needs to sit inside the stronger part of the
scrim ramp.

Gold as *large display type on dark navy* passes contrast comfortably and is used
deliberately. The brand rule that gold is never body text still holds — that rule is
about small text on light.

  anything runs as an ad.

  `claude/momentum-bot-branding-u34449` (PR #378), with its own `brand/momentum-bot.svg`
  mascot. It has been rendering and uploading to Drive independently. Two mascots and
