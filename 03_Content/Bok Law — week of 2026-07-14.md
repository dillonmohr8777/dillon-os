## Wednesday Wisdom — July 16

**Topic:** When kids ask about divorce

Children notice tension long before you sit them down. They may not ask directly. They might act out, cling more, or withdraw.

If you're ready to talk:

• Keep it age-appropriate. Young kids need reassurance that both parents still love them. Teens may want more honesty about logistics.
• Never put them in the middle. Avoid asking them to carry messages or choose sides.
• It's okay to say "I don't have all the answers yet." Stability matters more than perfection.

If you're not ready, that's valid too. A family therapist or your attorney can help you plan the conversation.

Your kids don't need you to be unbreakable. They need to know they're not alone in this.

## Turn the Page Thursday — July 17

**Topic:** Rebuilding routines after separation

Morning coffee. Sunday grocery runs. Who picks up from practice. Routines gave your family rhythm. Separation disrupts all of it.

Start small. Pick one anchor habit and protect it this week. Maybe it's breakfast with the kids before school. Maybe it's a walk after dinner.

New routines won't feel like the old ones at first. That's normal. The goal isn't to replicate what was. It's to build something steady enough that everyone knows what comes next.

Turning the page means designing a new normal, one week at a time.

## Family Fridays — July 18

**Topic:** Pittsburgh summer on a co-parenting schedule

Pittsburgh summers are short. Pool days, Kennywood trips, late sunsets at Schenley — you want your kids to enjoy all of it.

When schedules split weekends, plan early:

• Share the summer activity calendar before camps fill up.
• Split costs on big tickets upfront so nobody's surprised.
• Let the kids suggest one "must do" each — and honor it if the schedule allows.

Summer memories shouldn't depend on which house they're at. They should depend on showing up present, wherever you are.
