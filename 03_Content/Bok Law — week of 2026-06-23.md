## Wednesday Wisdom — June 25

**Topic:** Summer custody schedules and co-parent communication

When school lets out, custody schedules often shift. That transition can feel stressful even when both parents want what is best for the kids.

A few things that help:

• Put the summer schedule in writing before the first week of break. Even a simple shared calendar reduces last-minute confusion.
• Agree on how you'll handle travel, camps, and last-minute changes. A quick text is fine, but big decisions deserve a real conversation.
• Keep the kids out of the middle. They should not be messengers for schedule changes or adult frustrations.

If you're navigating a new summer arrangement after separation, mediation can help you build a plan that works for everyone without a courtroom fight. You don't have to figure it out alone.

## Turn the Page Thursday — June 26

**Topic:** New chapters after a difficult season

Some seasons of life ask more of you than you thought you had. A long divorce. A custody battle that drained your energy. A year where everything felt harder than it should.

Turning the page does not mean pretending the hard parts did not happen. It means you get to decide what comes next.

Maybe this summer is the first time you've planned something just for yourself in years. Maybe it's the first summer your kids see both parents at peace. Maybe it's simply the first week you slept through the night in months.

Whatever your chapter looks like, you earned the right to write it on your own terms.

## Family Fridays — June 27

**Topic:** Pittsburgh summer with kids

Pittsburgh summers are made for simple family moments. A morning at the Strip District for breakfast. An afternoon at Schenley Park. Free concerts at Point State Park when the weather cooperates.

You do not need a perfect plan or a big budget. Kids remember the small stuff: who was there, who laughed with them, who showed up.

If co-parenting makes summer logistics complicated, focus on quality over quantity. One fully present afternoon beats a rushed week of distracted time.

Here's to a Pittsburgh summer full of small moments that matter.
