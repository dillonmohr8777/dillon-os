## Wednesday Wisdom — July 30, 2026

**Topic:** Co-parenting and back-to-school transitions

**Caption:**

August is right around the corner, and for many Pittsburgh families, that means new schedules, new teachers, and a fresh set of logistics to figure out.

If you're co-parenting, back-to-school season can feel like a stress test. Who buys the supplies? Who handles registration? What happens when one parent's work schedule shifts and pickup times no longer line up?

Here's what we see help families most:

• **Talk early.** Don't wait until the week before school starts. A short conversation in late July about calendars, supply lists, and who handles what can prevent a lot of last-minute friction.

• **Keep the kids out of the middle.** School decisions should stay between the adults. Children shouldn't be messengers for schedule changes or supply runs.

• **Be flexible where you can.** Teachers change, activities get added, and buses run late. A little grace on both sides goes a long way when the routine is still settling in.

• **Put it in writing when it helps.** If your parenting plan doesn't address school-year specifics, a simple addendum or email trail confirming who handles what can save confusion later.

Back-to-school doesn't have to be a battleground. With a little planning and a focus on what your kids actually need, two households can still feel like one team when it matters most.

*This post is for general educational purposes only and is not legal advice.*

## Turn the Page Thursday — July 31, 2026

**Topic:** Permission to start over

**Caption:**

Sometimes the hardest part of a life transition isn't the paperwork or the logistics. It's giving yourself permission to actually turn the page.

You might catch yourself waiting for a sign that it's okay to feel hopeful again. Or wondering if moving forward means you're leaving something important behind.

It doesn't.

Starting a new chapter doesn't erase where you've been. It just means you're choosing not to let one season define every season that comes after it.

Maybe that looks like rearranging a room so it feels like yours again. Maybe it's saying yes to a coffee with a friend you haven't seen in months. Maybe it's simply waking up and deciding that today counts.

You don't need anyone's approval to begin again. You just need to believe that what's ahead can be different, and that different can be good.

## Family Fridays — August 1, 2026

**Topic:** Summer in Pittsburgh with kids

**Caption:**

There's still time to squeeze the last drops out of Pittsburgh summer, and you don't need a big budget or a packed calendar to make memories.

A few local favorites families keep coming back to:

• **Point State Park** — splash in the fountain, grab a bite from a food truck, and watch boats on the rivers. Free, easy, and always a hit with little ones.

• **Carnegie Library story times** — cool indoor option on a hot afternoon. Check your branch's schedule for what's running in late July and early August.

• **Farmers markets** — Lawrenceville, Market Square, or your neighborhood spot. Let the kids pick something new to try. Peaches are peaking right about now.

• **Evening walks in your own neighborhood** — chase fireflies, count porch flags, wave at the neighbors. Sometimes the best Friday night is the simple one.

However your family spends these last weeks of summer, we hope they're full of small moments that stick.

Happy Family Friday, Pittsburgh.
