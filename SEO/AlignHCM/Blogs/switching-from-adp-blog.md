# Switching From ADP: A Mid-Market Migration Playbook

## Meta Description
Switching from ADP? Here is the playbook for mid-market employers: when to move, what to watch, and how to avoid the classic migration mistakes.

## Content

Switching from ADP is a bigger decision than most vendors admit during the sales cycle. ADP is embedded in your benefits, your 401(k), your GL, your reporting, and sometimes your audit workpapers. Ripping it out without a playbook is how migrations become rescue projects.

Here is the approach we use at Align HCM when mid market clients decide it is time to move.

## When Switching From ADP Is the Right Move

Good reasons:
- Reporting cannot support leadership decisions
- Renewal pricing outpaced value
- Module gaps force point solutions you pay to integrate
- Service response times degraded during a migration or acquisition
- Platform roadmap no longer matches where your company is going

Bad reasons:
- One bad support ticket
- A competitor's aggressive pitch
- An internal champion wants to run the RFP

## When to Stay Put

If your Workforce Now instance is clean, your internal admin is strong, and your pain is localized to one module, optimization usually beats migration. Switching from ADP should be a last resort after targeted fixes fail.

## The Migration Playbook

### Phase 1: Confirm the business case
Model three-year TCO for staying versus leaving. Include implementation, parallel payroll, change management, and a realistic productivity dip. If the new platform does not clear the bar by 15 percent or more, reconsider.

### Phase 2: Select the destination
Run a structured evaluation. Typical finalists include UKG Pro, Dayforce, Paylocity, and Paycor depending on workforce profile.

### Phase 3: Lock the data foundation
Export everything from ADP before the contract conversation gets tense. Employee records, pay history, tax filings, benefit elections, time data, GL mappings. Validate it.

### Phase 4: Plan the calendar around payroll cycles
Never cut over mid-quarter unless you enjoy reconciliation. Ideal cutovers are first pay period of a new quarter, ideally Q1 for clean W-2 accounting.

### Phase 5: Parallel payroll twice minimum
Three for complex payrolls. Do not compress this step to save two weeks. You will spend those two weeks explaining variances to finance.

### Phase 6: Cutover and hypercare
Run the new platform and keep ADP accessible in read-only mode for 90 days. You will need it.

### Phase 7: Decommission
Export final data, confirm tax filing coverage through year end, terminate the ADP contract on the correct notice window.

## Risks Nobody Warns You About

**Tax filing handoff.** Year-end straddling a migration creates the most post-go-live pain of any step. Plan it in writing.

**Benefits carrier feeds.** Reconnecting carriers to a new platform takes longer than vendors estimate. Start early.

**GL mapping rework.** Finance will ask for the exact same reports in the exact same format on day one. Deliver that or expect friction.

**Employee experience cliff.** Logins, pay stubs, PTO balances all change the same week. Over-communicate.
