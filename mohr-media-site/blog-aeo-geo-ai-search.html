AEO & GEO: Get Cited by ChatGPT & AI Search in 2026

Search is moving from ten blue links to single AI answers - here is how to become the source those answers cite.

By Dillon Mohr · Jun 18, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR

Answer engines like ChatGPT and AI search now hand customers one answer citing just a few sources. AEO and GEO are how you become one of them - through structured data, clear Q&A content, entity clarity, quotable writing, off-site mentions, and current facts. Do it now, while most competitors still aren't.

Search is splitting in two. On one side, the old web of ten blue links you scroll and click. On the other, a growing set of answer engines - ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot - that read the web for your customer and hand back a single answer. If your business is not part of that answer, you are invisible to a fast-growing slice of your market. This post explains AEO and GEO in plain terms and gives you the concrete moves to become a source these tools cite.

What are AEO and GEO?

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of structuring your content so answer engines can lift a clear, correct answer straight from your page. Think featured snippets, voice assistants, and the direct responses at the top of a search result.

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) goes a step further. It is about getting large language models - the systems behind ChatGPT and AI search - to understand your business well enough to cite you as a trusted source when they generate an answer from scratch.

Traditional SEO asks, "How do I rank for this keyword?" AEO and GEO ask a different question: "How do I become the answer?" The mechanics overlap, but the goal has shifted from earning a click to earning a citation.

Why does this matter right now?

Because the behavior already changed. People no longer type three keywords and scan a page of links. They ask a full question and expect one answer. When an AI tool answers that question, it names a handful of sources - and everyone else is left out entirely.

This is winner-take-most. A blue-link results page has room for ten businesses. An AI answer often cites two or three. If you are not one of them, you do not get a smaller share of the traffic - you get none of it. Getting cited early, while most of your competitors still treat this as a novelty, is the cheapest it will ever be.

How do AI engines decide who to cite?

They are not guessing. Answer engines favor sources that are easy to parse, clearly about a specific thing, and corroborated elsewhere. In practice, that comes down to a few levers you can actually pull:

  • Structured data. Schema markup (Organization, Product, Article, FAQ, HowTo) tells the machine exactly what your page is, who published it, and what each piece of content means - no interpretation required.
  • Clear question-and-answer content. Pages built around real questions, with the answer stated plainly in the first sentence or two, are the easiest thing in the world for an engine to quote.
  • Entity clarity. The engine needs to know who you are as a distinct entity - your name, what you do, where you operate - and see it stated consistently everywhere you appear.
  • Quotable writing. Short, self-contained, factual sentences travel well. A sentence that only makes sense with three paragraphs of context around it does not get lifted.
  • Mentions across the web. Citations, directory listings, reviews, and references on sites the model already trusts act as corroboration. One page saying you are the expert is a claim; twenty sources agreeing is a fact.
  • Current facts. Stale prices, old hours, and dead links get you dropped. Models increasingly weight freshness, and a page that contradicts reality is a page they stop trusting.

What tactics actually get you cited?

Here is what we build into client sites at Mohr Media - and what you can start on this week.

Answer the question in the first sentence

Lead every key page and section with a direct answer, then explain. "The average roof replacement in Austin costs $8,000 to $15,000." State it, then support it. Engines pull the statement; humans read the support.

Add FAQ schema to your important pages

An FAQ section marked up with FAQPage schema does double duty: it answers real customer questions in the exact question-and-answer format engines love, and it hands them clean, structured data to quote. This is one of the highest-leverage, lowest-effort moves available.

Make your entity unmistakable

Use Organization schema. Keep your name, address, phone, and description identical across your site, Google Business Profile, and every directory. Publish a substantive About page that states plainly who you are and what you do. Consistency is what turns a name into a recognized entity.

Earn mentions off your own site

Get listed in reputable directories, quoted in industry publications, and reviewed by real customers. Off-site corroboration is often the deciding factor in whether a model trusts you enough to name you.

Keep it current

Review key facts on a schedule. Update prices, hours, offerings, and dates. Add a visible "last updated" date to cornerstone pages. Freshness is a trust signal, and it is one most businesses ignore.

Does traditional SEO still matter?

Yes - it is the foundation, not the ceiling. Fast, crawlable, well-structured pages with genuine authority still win. AEO and GEO do not replace SEO; they sit on top of it. The businesses that dominate AI answers in 2026 are the ones that got the fundamentals right and then made their content machine-readable and quotable on purpose.

The good news: most of your competitors are not doing this yet. The window is open.

The old goal was to rank. The new goal is to be the answer. Build for both, because for now, you have to.

Where do you start?

Start with your highest-intent pages - the ones where a buyer is close to a decision. Add clear Q&A content, mark it up with FAQ and Organization schema, tighten your entity details, and make sure every fact on the page is true today. That alone will put you ahead of most of your market.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between AEO and GEO?
AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) focuses on structuring content so answer engines can extract a clear, correct answer from your page - think featured snippets and voice results. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) focuses on getting large language models like ChatGPT to understand and cite your business as a trusted source when they generate answers. AEO is about being extractable; GEO is about being credible enough to name.
Do I still need traditional SEO in 2026?
Yes. SEO is the foundation AEO and GEO build on. Fast, crawlable, authoritative pages still win, and AI engines draw heavily from well-ranked content. The shift is additive: you keep the SEO fundamentals and then make your content machine-readable and quotable so answer engines cite you directly.
How does ChatGPT decide which businesses to cite?
AI engines favor sources that are easy to parse (clear structure and schema markup), clearly about a specific entity, corroborated by mentions elsewhere on the web, and factually current. Pages that answer real questions in plain, self-contained sentences are the easiest for a model to lift and cite.
How quickly can I see results from AEO and GEO work?
Some wins are fast - adding FAQ schema and clear Q&A content can improve extractability within weeks as engines recrawl your pages. Building entity trust and off-site corroboration takes longer, typically a few months. Starting now matters because most competitors have not, so the cost of getting cited is lower today than it will be later.
