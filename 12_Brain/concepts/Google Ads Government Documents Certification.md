# Google Ads Government Documents Certification

**Summary:** Pennsylvania tag-agency ads for titles, plates, and registration stay disapproved until Google certifies the advertiser as an authorized provider; PennDOT lists agents by name, address, and phone only and will not add a website field, so after a template denial the packet is a resubmit that points at the live government directory pages plus PennDOT’s written confirmation.

## What Google actually requires

Ads that promote **direct acquisition** of vehicle owner registration, license plates, titles, or driver-license products sit inside Google’s [Government documents and services](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13156083) policy.

To run those ads as a private tag agency:

1. Apply as **authorized, non-government provider** at [godos_certification](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/contact/godos_certification) (or Admin → Policy → Account → Apply for certification if that control is on the account).
2. Complete advertiser verification if it is not already done. Legal name and address on the form must match verification.
3. Wait for the certificate on the account (Google’s stated window is about 10–12 business days after verification), then appeal the disapproved ads.

The form asks for the advertiser domain **and** the official government URL that **links to that domain** and names the business as authorized. A certificate of authorization, Hostinger domain paperwork, and a state business filing do not replace that link.

The United States is **not** on the vehicle-registration or license-plate exclusion lists. Do not file as a regional exclusion for a Pennsylvania tag agency.

## What PennDOT actually publishes

PennDOT’s [Authorized Agents and Messengers](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dmv/resources/business-partners/authorized-agents-messengers) page points at PDF directories. Those PDFs list name, street, city, and phone. There is **no website column** for any agent.

Rechecked 2026-08-15: both live PDFs still list Tags 2 Go LLC at 6001 Torresdale Ave, Philadelphia. The online-agent PDF also prints a phone. Neither PDF, and not the pa.gov landing page, hyperlinks the advertiser domain.

That means:

- DAS / dealers.penndot.gov cannot add a public URL field that does not exist.
- The Agent Support resource account handles **title and registration transactions**, not Google Ads packets and not public-directory websites.
- Contract Administration / DAS can update the **business name and address** that appear in the PDF. They **cannot** add a website. Bureau of Support Services confirmed in writing on 2026-08-14 that DAS has no URL field, they do not collect that information, and the public list is address and phone only. Do not ask them again for a hyperlink.
- The DVS Update newsletter/publications inbox is the published mailbox for agent bulletins. Use it only as a **routing** ask. Do not use it to reopen a website-field request after Contract Administration has answered.

A government-managed partner directory is the kind of source Google’s October 2026 update still treats as valid authorization **if** it identifies the provider. Pennsylvania’s directory identifies the agent and **will not** hyperlink the domain. After a template denial, resubmit with the unwrapped pa.gov page and the two PDF URLs, plus PennDOT’s written “address and phone only” confirmation, and ask for a manual review. Do not invent a government backlink. Do not keep emailing PennDOT for a URL field.

Starting **2026-10-05**, Google will reject commercial contracts, trade licenses, and registry entries as substitutes. Get the certification decision in before that date if possible. Policy: [October 2026 update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/17260489).

## What happened on the first filing

Google denied the 2026-08-14 authorized-provider filing on 2026-08-15 with the standard eligibility template and asked for a resubmit that includes the eligibility-page link. That is a first-pass miss on the domain-hyperlink rule, not a finding that the business is unauthorized. The live government directories still name the agent. The next packet is those directory URLs, not a new PennDOT ask and not an out-of-scope claim.

## Operator sequence

1. Confirm the live PennDOT PDF lists the current legal name and address.
2. Submit **one** `godos_certification` application as authorized provider. If Google denies with the eligibility template, resubmit once with the unwrapped government directory URLs and PennDOT’s written no-URL confirmation. Do not file a third copy unless Google asks again.
3. Keep title/registration campaigns paused until the certificate appears.
4. After approval, appeal the disapproved assets and relaunch Search only, geo-limited to the authorization area, with a landing page that discloses the business is an independent authorized provider and not a government site. Google will add a “Not a government website” disclosure on Search ads for non-government providers.
5. Do not mix notary/office keywords into the restricted campaign. Do not pivot the paid program to extras unless the client asks for that.
6. Do not email the client while chasing the certificate. Outreach is the `godos_certification` form and Google Ads support on that case. After Contract Administration answers, stop asking PennDOT for a website field. Do not reply on title-support threads that already copy the client.

## What not to do

- Do not keep emailing PennDOT Agent Support with Google Ads evidence.
- Do not ask PennDOT Contract Administration, DAS, or publications to add a website field after they have said the public list is address and phone only.
- Do not send this packet to DVS press officers or Central Communications.
- Do not use the public Motor Vehicle Questions form for a directory-website request.
- Do not tell the client a Hostinger domain certificate satisfies Google’s government-link rule.
- Do not claim the ads are “out of scope” while the live site sells registration, titles, plates, or driver-license products.
