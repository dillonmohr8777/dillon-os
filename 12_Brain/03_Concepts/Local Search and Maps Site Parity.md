# Local Search and Maps-to-Site Parity

Local search is a trust and availability problem before it is a keyword
problem. The system must agree on who the business is, what it offers, where it
can serve, and how a person can take the next step.

## Canonical local record

For each location or service-area business, verify:

```yaml
canonical_name: ""
primary_category: ""
secondary_categories: []
address_or_service_area: ""
phone: ""
hours: ""
appointment_or_quote_url: ""
services: []
service_areas: []
maps_url: ""
website_url: ""
tracking_numbers: []
last_verified: 2026-08-01
```

Tracking numbers must preserve a stable canonical number where directories and
schema require consistency. Record the intended routing instead of letting
temporary campaign numbers fragment identity.

## Parity surfaces

Audit agreement across:

- the real operating business;
- Google Business Profile and Bing Places;
- homepage, contact, location, service, and footer content;
- structured data;
- appointment, quote, call, and form destinations;
- high-value directories and industry profiles;
- social profiles;
- reviews and review responses;
- ads and landing pages; and
- customer-agent or chatbot answers.

Parity means factual agreement, not identical copy everywhere.

## Local page architecture

### Location page

Use for a real staffed location or clearly defined operating presence. Include
the real address or service model, hours, directions context, services,
location-specific proof, team or imagery, accessibility, and the correct
contact path.

### Service-area page

Use only where the business actually serves the market and can add distinct
information: travel boundaries, project examples, regulations, climate,
inventory, response time, pricing context, neighborhoods, or local proof.

### Service page

Own the service intent. Explain fit, process, inclusions, exclusions, proof,
cost or timeline context, and next action. Link to relevant markets without
creating near-duplicate city pages.

### Project or proof page

Show real work, location context, constraints, solution, imagery, and outcome.
These pages create evidence that generic local pages cannot.

## Maps-to-site trust checklist

- Primary category matches the actual service and site positioning.
- Services and service areas are current.
- Hours, phone, and contact routes work.
- The website does not contradict Maps.
- Real images show the place, people, work, products, or experience.
- Review themes are reflected in proof and FAQs without copying private data.
- Landing pages keep the same business identity and geography as the ad/profile.
- LocalBusiness or Organization schema matches visible content.
- Directions, call, booking, and forms are measurable and usable on mobile.
- Duplicate, closed, moved, or stale profiles are identified.

## Review intelligence

Reviews reveal demand and trust language. Compile themes, not raw customer
archives:

- problem and desired outcome;
- service or product chosen;
- geography;
- decision criteria;
- objections or anxiety;
- proof customers value;
- staff, speed, cleanliness, communication, price, or quality themes; and
- repeated gaps or service failures.

Use themes to improve pages, scripts, operations, and FAQs. Never fabricate or
selectively misrepresent reviews.

## Local keyword and question model

Combine:

- service + market;
- problem + near me;
- cost + market;
- best-fit comparison;
- open now or availability;
- reviews and reputation;
- process, timing, permits, eligibility, or insurance;
- neighborhoods and landmarks when genuinely relevant; and
- branded navigation and wrong-company confusion.

Map each cluster to one canonical page and one conversion path. Do not build a
page for every place-name permutation.

## Local AEO/GEO

Answer engines need the same local facts humans do:

- a concise business definition;
- services and exclusions;
- exact location or service area;
- hours and availability;
- pricing or quote process when supportable;
- real proof and imagery;
- review and reputation context;
- current profiles and citations; and
- a working next action.

Track Maps/site parity, fixed local prompts, Bing grounding queries, AI
citations, calls, directions, forms, appointments, estimates, and booked work
as separate metrics.

## Measurement

- profile completeness and contradiction count;
- local query impressions and clicks;
- Maps actions, with definitions and known limitations;
- landing-page engagement;
- call and form delivery;
- qualified estimate or appointment rate;
- booked work and value;
- review velocity and theme mix;
- citation and grounding-query coverage; and
- change-to-outcome timeline.

## Failure modes

- Keyword-stuffed business names or categories.
- City-page sprawl with no distinct local value.
- Stock imagery where trust requires real proof.
- A tracking number or landing page that changes the perceived business.
- Counting directions, calls, or forms as qualified outcomes without
  reconciliation.
- Publishing an address, service area, hours, or claim without verification.
- Treating one directory submission as authority.
