# Anti-AI Client Operating Model

**One line:** when a client is reported as anti-AI, the response is authentic
client voice + human approval before publish + high craft — **not** concealing
tooling from the client.

## The situation

A decision-maker on the client side is reported as strongly anti-AI, so
deliverables must not read as generic AI output. On the Pritzker Law Group
engagement this attribute is **Dillon-reported and not independently verified
against the client's own words** — see
[[12_Brain/01_Captures/Communications/2026-09-03 - Pritzker anti-AI stance and SEO directive]].

## The model

Four mechanisms, all of which a regulated client (a law firm, a medical
practice, a financial advisor) already needs for its own fact-safety:

1. **Authentic voice.** Draft from a real voice-capture profile built only from
   client-approved writing samples. Never fabricate the profile. Until real
   samples land, dependent automations stop and flag. Mechanism home:
   [[01_Clients/Pritzker Law Group/voice-profile]].
2. **Human approval before publish.** Every claim, disclaimer, and CTA passes
   the client's named final approval owner before anything goes live. This is an
   approval gate, not a formality.
3. **Fact-safe.** Only claims verifiable from first-party client sources ship.
   No invented guests, episodes, dates, platforms, audience size, outcomes,
   awards, testimonials, legal advice, sponsor benefits, metrics, or search
   volumes. Anything unknown is marked TO CONFIRM.
4. **High craft.** Held to a quality bar that makes the work genuinely
   human-owned.

## What this model is not

It is **not** a concealment strategy. Nothing in this model hides tooling from
the client, misrepresents who did the work, or answers "did you use AI?"
dishonestly. The output is human-owned because a human owns the voice, the
approval, and the quality bar — not because the tooling is hidden.

Framing this work as "hiding AI from the client" is a misreading and must never
be written into a skill, plan, or client-facing artifact.

## Known risk: AI-forward artifacts

Proposing a visibly AI-branded artifact to a reported-anti-AI decision-maker
(for example an always-on AI voice agent) is a **relationship risk**, independent
of whether the artifact is good. Prefer plain, non-AI-forward mechanisms as the
primary path; anything AI-forward requires explicit client consent and should be
raised as a risk, not slipped in as a config choice.
