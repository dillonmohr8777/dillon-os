# Search Intent and Topic Architecture

Intent is the job a person is trying to complete. Topic architecture is the
page and link system that helps them complete it while proving the brand's
authority.

## Intent taxonomy

Use enough detail to change the asset decision.

| Intent | User job | Strong formats | Primary outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learn | Understand a concept or category | definition, guide, glossary, explainer | deeper exploration or subscription |
| Solve | Fix a specific problem | diagnostic, checklist, how-to, troubleshooting guide | self-resolution or service evaluation |
| Research | Build a mental model before a decision | pillar guide, original data, framework | return visit, citation, or lead nurture |
| Compare | Choose among alternatives | versus page, alternatives, decision matrix | qualified evaluation |
| Commercial | Evaluate providers, products, cost, or fit | service page, cost guide, reviews, case study | inquiry or sales conversation |
| Transactional | Complete an action now | booking, product, application, quote, location page | completed conversion |
| Local | Find an available nearby solution | service-area page, location page, GBP-aligned page | call, direction, appointment, estimate |
| Navigational | Reach a known brand, product, or person | home, contact, login, branded entity page | successful destination |
| Creation | Produce an artifact or plan | template, calculator, prompt, tool | tool use, download, or qualified lead |
| Live or current | Find changing status, event, availability, or news | current inventory, event, update, status page | time-sensitive action |

A query can carry more than one intent. Choose a primary intent and record the
secondary one rather than forcing every phrase into one bucket.

## The topic unit

A durable topic cluster combines:

```text
entity + audience + problem or goal + context + decision stage
```

Example:

```text
Google Ads + local service owner + poor lead quality
+ incomplete CRM attribution + diagnostic/commercial stage
```

This unit is more stable than a keyword and more actionable than a broad topic.

## Page roles

### Pillar or definitive guide

Owns the complete subject and links to narrower jobs. It should define the
entity or problem, explain the system, cite evidence, answer major questions,
and point to the correct next step.

### Service or solution page

Explains who the service is for, the problem solved, process, boundaries,
proof, expected outcome, and conversion path. It is not a blog post with a
button added.

### Supporting answer

Resolves one narrower question, objection, comparison, location, or use case.
It should add distinct value and link back to the canonical subject.

### Proof asset

Case study, original data, calculator, demo, benchmark, testimonial, or method
that gives humans and engines a reason to trust and cite the brand.

### Conversion asset

Booking, lead form, assessment, download, store locator, or other action page.
It should minimize ambiguity and preserve attribution.

## Cluster design

For each cluster, map:

1. the canonical entity or problem page;
2. questions required to understand it;
3. comparison and alternative decisions;
4. cost, timing, risk, and implementation questions;
5. local or industry variants with genuine distinct value;
6. proof assets;
7. tools, templates, or calculators;
8. the conversion destination; and
9. the update cadence.

Do not create a location or vertical page by replacing place and industry
names in otherwise identical copy. A valid variant needs distinct service
availability, proof, examples, constraints, imagery, or local information.

## Internal linking model

Links should express the relationship:

- definition -> process;
- process -> service;
- service -> proof;
- proof -> conversion;
- question -> canonical guide;
- local page -> relevant service and proof; and
- related concept -> next decision.

Use descriptive anchor context. Avoid mechanical link blocks that do not help a
reader understand where they are going.

## Cannibalization test

Before creating a page, ask:

1. Does an existing page already satisfy the same primary job?
2. Would the searcher expect meaningfully different content?
3. Can each page have distinct proof and conversion logic?
4. Can the internal linking explain the hierarchy?
5. Is there enough evidence to maintain both pages?

If not, improve the existing canonical page.

## Content brief contract

Every brief should specify:

- audience and primary job;
- primary and secondary intent;
- topic/entity and boundaries;
- source-backed questions;
- direct answer or promise;
- required evidence and examples;
- page role and target URL;
- internal links in and out;
- structured data that truthfully matches visible content;
- conversion path;
- distribution plan; and
- measurement and refresh trigger.

Use [[_templates/Content Cluster Brief|Content Cluster Brief]].

## Measure architecture, not volume

Track:

- high-value intents with a canonical page;
- orphan or competing pages;
- query-to-page stability;
- impressions and citations by cluster;
- assisted and direct qualified outcomes;
- refresh debt;
- internal path to proof and conversion; and
- the percentage of pages with a real purpose beyond acquiring traffic.

## Failure modes

- Keyword cannibalization disguised as topical authority.
- Huge pillar pages with no usable navigation or distinct answers.
- Thin local or industry programmatic pages.
- FAQ blocks that repeat the page instead of resolving real questions.
- Internal links optimized for anchor text rather than comprehension.
- Content disconnected from an offer, proof, or next action.
- Publishing more when the actual gap is weak authority or distribution.
