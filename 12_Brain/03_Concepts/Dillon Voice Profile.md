# Dillon Voice Profile

One lesson: **Dillon sounds like a smart operator texting a colleague, not like an agency.** Copy that
adds polish in the direction of "marketing" moves away from his voice, not toward it.

Evidence and raw counts: [[2026-09-02 Dillon Voice Corpus]].

Built from 479 substantive Slack messages he personally typed between 2026-01-19 and 2026-09-02,
filtered to exclude agent and automation output posted under his token. Average message: 182
characters.

## The headline finding

Across 479 messages he wrote **zero** instances of: elevate, unlock, empower, seamless, robust,
innovative, transform, synergy, cutting-edge, best-in-class, holistic, game-changer, circle back,
touch base, deliverables, stakeholders, ecosystem. One "leverage". One "bandwidth". Two "solutions".

He does not write marketing language. He writes what happened and what is next. Any copy containing
the words above is not in his voice, no matter how well constructed.

## Two registers

He runs two, and they are genuinely different. Pick by audience, never blend mid-paragraph.

### Register A - peer

DMs, `#ai-tech-news`, teammates he likes. Fast, warm, profane, funny.

- "Dawg that sounds fun as shit"
- "I'm so fucking bored today. I've literally caught up on every single thing I could catch up on"
- "no problem is too big with GPT 5.6 SOL on max"
- "I go hard for the people that go hard for me"
- "Nothing better than going on our long walk speaking into your mobile coding platform like it's Jarvis"

Traits: run-on sentences, few commas, stretched letters for emphasis ("Dudeeee", "reallllly",
"SO MUCH"), variable-length "hahaha", heavy laughing emoji, `lol` 33x, `forsure` (always one word),
`lyk` / `lmk` / `bc` / `dw` / `fs` / `tho` / `w`. Constant "like" as filler. Profanity is real but
sparse - 9 instances total, always for emphasis, never aggression.

### Register B - operator

Client channels, handoffs, reports, anything with a number in it. This is the register that should
drive brand and outreach copy.

- "August report attached. Google delivered $504.49 in spend, 138 clicks, and a 5.77% click through rate. Next is matching inquiries to appointment outcomes before budget changes."
- "I own the canonical frontend and UX requirements, while your team owns the backend APIs."
- "No verified advertising spend source is mapped, so no performance claim is included."
- "Please enforce admin access through backend RBAC, not only by hiding the route on the frontend."
- "Do not resend that credential in Slack or ordinary email."

Traits: number first, then the comparison, then the next action. Numbered lists when he needs things
from someone. Explicit scope boundaries. He states what he did **not** verify rather than papering
over it.

## Structural moves that appear in both registers

1. **Front-load the outcome.** He opens with the result, not the setup. Top openers: "Sounds good",
   "Okay great", "August report attached", "Let me", "Thank you".
2. **End on the specific next step.** "Next is..." / "I'll..." (159 instances of `I'll`). He almost
   never ends a work message without saying who does what next.
3. **Name the gap instead of bluffing.** "no true KPIs to report, issues are client won't respond."
   "I don't currently have confirmed API documentation or environment access."
4. **Ask in numbered items so nobody guesses.** "I just need three final items from you so I don't
   guess: 1... 2... 3..."
5. **Offer help unprompted.** "let me know" 22x, "happy to help" 7x, "you got it" 6x, "I got you" 4x.
   This is his most consistent relational move across all nine months.
6. **Credit people by name in public.** He thanks specific people in channel constantly.

## Mechanics

| Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Hyphens, never em dashes | 33 " - " vs 5 em dashes; matches the Kimi design brief anti-slop rule |
| Contractions always | 159 `I'll` vs 15 `I will` |
| Exclamation points are real but not constant | 0.5 per message; 35 messages end on one |
| "forsure" is one word | 8 of 9 instances |
| Intensifiers over adjectives | "really" 50x, "super" 14x, "definitely" 14x - he intensifies plain words rather than reaching for fancy ones |
| Apologizes and self-corrects openly | "sorry" 22x, "my bad" 6x |
| "just" is his most-used softener | 102 instances |

## Writing as him: the test

Before shipping copy in his voice, check:

- [ ] Could this sentence appear in a Slack message he actually sent? If it reads like a website, cut it.
- [ ] Is there a number, or an honest statement that there isn't one yet?
- [ ] Does it end on a specific next action with an owner?
- [ ] Zero words from the jargon list above?
- [ ] Hyphens not em dashes, contractions on?
- [ ] For Register B: would he be comfortable being held to every claim in it?

## What this profile does not cover

Slack only, workplace only, 2026. It does not include how he writes to strangers cold, to prospects,
or in long-form. Cold-outreach voice is an extrapolation from Register B and should be validated
against real replies before scaling. Related: [[Prospect to Build to Outreach Pipeline]].
