## 1. Executive decision

The campaign does not need more polished assets before it starts. It needs a human-led distribution engine.

The strongest idea is already in the source material: a chief marketing officer who spent years marketing everybody else finally tries to earn attention for the rap album he was afraid to release. That is the series. The music, the Erie and Pittsburgh identity, King Keev, Mac Miller's influence, fatherhood, and the day-job versus night-booth split give the series depth.

The existing 35 statics, 6 Split Series cards, 27 motion assets, 8 real photos, EPK, website, blog, and email sequence are the supporting library. They provide consistency, proof, and conversion surfaces. They should not be the majority of discovery posts. A waveform is packaging. Dillon's face, voice, choices, uncertainty, and listener reactions are the content people can attach themselves to.

### The three big bets

1. **Make the launch a public experiment, not a string of announcements.** The recurring promise is: watch a CMO try to become a rapper for real and show the work honestly. Every week needs Delusion Diary, First Listen, The Split, Making It, and 814 or Pittsburgh stories.
2. **Use one production engine across platforms, but publish natively.** Record one useful 45 to 60 minute batch each week. Turn it into four strong vertical videos, two supporting feed posts, daily story material, one live session, and platform-specific text. Cross-post the idea, not a watermarked export or identical caption.
3. **Scale only proven messages.** Run organic creative tests first. Put money behind the top quartile of posts based on retention, shares, profile visits, and gate signups. Do not pay to rescue weak content or buy playlist placement.

### The next 90 days in one sentence

Build a recognizable creator series, convert attention into an owned email audience, turn the first single into the first release event, and use weekly performance data to multiply only the hooks and stories that earn real listener action.

## 4. The content machine

- Five direct-to-camera hooks, each delivered twice.

## 5. Platform roles

### TikTok

This is the fastest hook-testing surface. Publish direct-to-camera stories, first listens, reply videos, studio fragments, and local identity posts. Keep the production native and human. Use comments as the next script queue. Once a post wins organically, its creator-style format can become a Spark Ad test while keeping engagement attached to the original post.

### Instagram

Reels handle discovery. Stories handle daily relationship and polls. Carousels provide saves and context. Lives deepen the connection. Pin three posts: who IMMOHRTAL is, the strongest song door, and the album or list invitation. Highlights should be START HERE, MUSIC, 814, MAKING IT, and LIVE.

### YouTube

Shorts carry the four weekly vertical stories. Titles should name the idea, not merely the track. The official video and future visualizers become long-form conversion destinations. Community posts and comment replies can recycle audience questions. Compare Shorts with Shorts, not with long-form videos, and track engaged views and whether viewers chose to watch rather than swiping.

### LinkedIn

This is not a side channel. The CMO angle is strongest here and can generate press, professional curiosity, and high-quality early listeners. Use the honest double-life story, lessons from marketing his own work, starting late, creative fear, and transparent results. Do not turn the music into a corporate case study. The vulnerability is the advantage.

### X and Threads

Use concise confessions, lines from the process, Erie and Pittsburgh observations, and live reactions. One weekly thread can unpack a track or the public experiment. Images should support the post, not substitute for a reason to care.

### Facebook

Cross-post Reels and Stories natively, then use Dillon's real network and relevant local communities carefully. No link dumping or group spam. A personal post about Erie, Pittsburgh, Keev, or starting late will travel better here than a generic album announcement.

### Spotify and Apple Music

These become active after distribution is confirmed. Claim the artist profiles, complete bios and images, add the existing Spotify Canvases, and use one universal smart link. Pitch the lead single through Spotify for Artists at least seven days before release with full context, promotion plans, collaborators, location, and story. Never pay for guaranteed playlist placement. Apple Music for Artists can generate Linkfire-powered links and social assets after the release is delivered and the profile can be claimed.

### Press, curators, and local ecosystem

Use the EPK and the story, not a mass blast. Segment outreach into Pittsburgh music, Erie and 814 press, marketing trades, independent hip-hop outlets, college radio, and legitimate curator platforms. Personalize the first paragraph and track every pitch, follow-up, response, and link. Do not use fake accounts, stream manipulation, or paid guaranteed placements.

## 8. Measurement and decision rules

The funnel is:

**Reach → engaged view → profile visit → site click → preview play → gate signup → pre-save or DSP visit → stream → save → follow → share**

### Creative metrics

- Chose to view versus swiped away where available.
- Average watch time and completion rate.
- Shares and saves per reached viewer.
- Comments that demonstrate story or music interest.
- Profile visits and follows per post.

### Business and fan metrics

- Link clicks by platform and campaign using UTMs.
- Preview plays.
- Gate signup conversion rate.
- Cost per gate signup once paid begins.
- Pre-saves when a real pre-save exists.
- Spotify and Apple listeners, saves, follows, playlist adds, and source of streams.
- Email open, click, reply, unsubscribe, and referral activity.

### Weekly decisions

- **Multiply:** top-quartile post on retention plus a meaningful action. Make three variants.
- **Keep learning:** good retention but weak action. Change the CTA or landing path.
- **Repackage:** weak opening but strong comments from people who stayed. Replace the first two seconds.
- **Stop:** weak retention and weak action after two clean tests.
- **Promote:** only an organic winner with verified conversion tracking and a clear next action.

Avoid arbitrary universal thresholds at the start. Establish the first four-week baseline, then compare each format to its own platform and format history.

## 9. Paid scaling ladder

### Stage 0: organic proof, $0 to $500 per month

Use the budget for basic production needs, captioning, and possibly an ESP. Run at least 12 to 20 human-led creative tests before meaningful paid spend. The goal is to find messages that work without buying attention.

### Stage 1: controlled tests, approximately $1,000 to $1,500 per month

- Boost only the top two or three organic videos.
- Start with Meta Reels and TikTok Spark Ads.
- Test roughly $10 to $20 per creative per day for five to seven days.
- Separate cold discovery from warm retargeting.
- Optimize to `gate_signup` before release and to a verified smart-link or artist action after release.
- Exclude existing signups from acquisition campaigns.

### Stage 2: repeatable scale, approximately $3,000 to $5,000 per month

Unlock only after multiple creative winners and a stable landing conversion rate exist. Increase spend gradually, keep 20 percent of budget for new creative tests, refresh hooks weekly, and cut ads when both retention and downstream conversion decline.

Paid media should amplify discovery and owned-audience growth. It should not be used to purchase streams blindly. A cheap video view with no profile visit, signup, save, or follow is not progress.

### Revenue
