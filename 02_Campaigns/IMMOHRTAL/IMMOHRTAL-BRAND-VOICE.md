# IMMOHRTAL Brand Voice

**Owner:** Dillon Mohr

**Role in market:** Founder and hands on CMO who can diagnose the marketing system, make the work, and explain what changes next.

## The voice in one sentence

Clear enough for an owner to trust, sharp enough for a CMO to respect, and specific enough that nobody mistakes it for generated marketing filler.

## Core position

IMMOHRTAL does not sell random tactics. It connects the website, search visibility, paid media, content, CRM, and supervised business agents into one working customer path.

Dillon speaks as an operator. He notices the real friction, names it plainly, shows the work, and gives the buyer one useful next step.

## Five voice traits

1. **Observant**
   Start with something true about the buyer, market, page, campaign, or process.

2. **Commercial**
   Connect the observation to leads, trust, sales conversations, wasted spend, or speed.

3. **Specific**
   Name the page, service, search behavior, handoff, or decision. Do not hide behind broad claims.

4. **Calmly confident**
   State the recommendation. Do not beg, posture, or manufacture urgency.

5. **Human**
   Use normal words, varied rhythm, and a real point of view. Sound like Dillon talking to another capable owner.

## The CMO structure

Use this order for most business writing:

1. What I noticed
2. Why it matters commercially
3. What I would change
4. What the buyer can inspect
5. One direct next step

## Copy rules

- Lead with the buyer's world, not IMMOHRTAL.
- Use one idea per paragraph.
- Prefer proof, examples, and visible work over adjectives.
- Give the reader one clear ask.
- Keep the first outreach email between 60 and 110 words before the signature.
- Treat the website concept as evidence, not as a trick or a finished replacement.
- Never promise rankings, revenue, leads, or conversions that have not been verified.
- Use first person when Dillon made or inspected something.
- Use contractions when they sound natural.
- End with a useful question, not a vague invitation.

## Language to use

- I noticed
- Here is the part I would fix first
- The current page makes this harder than it needs to be
- I built a direction you can inspect
- The goal is a clearer path into a real conversation
- Worth sending over the three changes I would prioritize?
- If this is already handled, I will leave it there
- Search, paid media, and the CRM should tell the same story
- The work should be visible before the pitch gets bigger

## Language to remove

- I hope this email finds you well
- I wanted to reach out
- We leverage cutting edge solutions
- In today's fast paced digital landscape
- Transform your online presence
- Unlock your full potential
- Revolutionize your marketing
- Robust, seamless, comprehensive, innovative, holistic
- Furthermore, moreover, in conclusion
- Just checking in
- Circling back
- Let me know if you are interested
- Any unsupported superlative or guaranteed outcome

## Outreach voice

Outreach should feel like a useful note from a prepared peer.

**Subject:** short, specific, and connected to the observation.

**Opening:** one verified observation about their current customer path.

**Middle:** show the concept or useful diagnosis and explain its purpose in one sentence.

**Ask:** request a reply, comparison, or permission to send the top three priorities. Do not ask for a long meeting in the first message.

**Close:** include a plain opt out and the compliant business footer once verified.

### First touch model

Hi [Name],

I noticed [specific verified observation]. It makes [commercial action] harder to find than it should be.

I built [specific deliverable] that gives [important service or proof] a clearer path into [quote, call, booking, or sale].

[Inspect the work]

Worth sending over the three changes I would prioritize first?

If you would rather I not follow up, just say the word.

## Social voice

Social content should teach from real work.

- One opinion
- One concrete example
- One practical takeaway
- One low pressure next step

Avoid generic inspiration, fake founder stories, and recycled marketing lists.

### Social example

Most websites do not have a traffic problem first. They have a next step problem.

The service is buried. The proof is scattered. The quote path appears after the buyer has already left.

Fix that sequence before buying more clicks.

## Website voice

Website copy should answer four questions quickly:

1. What do you fix?
2. Who is it for?
3. What work can I inspect?
4. What should I do next?

Use specific service names and public prices where approved. Keep custom work clearly custom scoped.

## Applied lessons from the copywriting canon

These are operating principles, not borrowed lines.

- **Claude Hopkins:** Treat marketing as measurable salesmanship. Be specific. Test the claim and the offer.
- **David Ogilvy:** Research before writing. Respect the reader. Make the headline carry useful information.
- **Eugene Schwartz:** Meet the buyer at the awareness level they already have. Channel existing desire instead of inventing it.
- **Robert Bly:** Make the value and response path clear. Remove language that does not help the reader decide.
- **John Caples:** Give headlines one job and test meaningful alternatives.
- **Joseph Sugarman:** Make each sentence earn the next sentence through curiosity and relevance.

## Final quality gate

Before copy ships, ask:

- Is the opening about the reader?
- Is every claim true and specific?
- Does the work sound like Dillon, not a committee?
- Is there one clear next step?
- Did we remove AI filler and fake urgency?
- Is outreach free of em dash and en dash punctuation?
- Is external delivery approved for this exact recipient and message?
