# Design System: With Not For AI — Human-Controlled Product Reveal

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The AI in the Palm"**

This system translates the current Apple Mac mini launch page's stillness, scale changes, and product-reveal choreography into With Not For's human-controlled AI worldview. `WNF.AI` arrives as the dominant object, moves through five authored material states, compresses into the exact orange robot, and resolves above a human palm before the source positioning line takes over. The page feels like a product launch because the implementation itself is the proof of craft.

After the 7.8-second opening, the page moves through single-idea viewports, a horizontal highlights sequence, a dedicated robot product-theatre chapter, sticky service chapters, a realistic Morning Briefing interaction, and an exact-logo particle finale. It remains unmistakably With Not For through its official logo, deep forest palette, practical source copy, and refusal to invent proof.

**Key Characteristics:**

- One rehearsed five-state opening animation, supported by purposeful scroll choreography rather than repeated fade-and-rise effects.
- Very large type, deliberate whitespace, and one dominant thought per fold.
- Forest, paper, and black fields punctuated by the orange robot.
- The opening palm plus editorial people photography make “with, not for” visibly human; the later human-led chapter contains no floating hand.
- Exactly three authored robot appearances: opening collaborator, product-theatre breakdown, and footer transformation seed.
- Brand-specific circuit, systems, team, policy, and adoption icons drawn from one stroke grammar, then animated with distinct motion signatures.
- Liquid glass reserved for interactive controls, capability lenses, and literal interface surfaces.
- The footer robot dissolves through six circuit routes into deterministic alpha-sampled particles, then locks to the exact official WNF logo.
- Full keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, and narrow-screen states.

## Colors

The palette is committed rather than timid: With Not For forest owns long sections, paper fields create product focus, and the orange robot is the only warm interruption.

### Primary

- **Working Forest** (`green`): primary buttons, active states, long chapter fields, and focusable product moments.
- **Deep Canopy** (`forest`): the human-control chapter and grounded dark-green fields.

### Secondary

- **Robot Orange** (`robot`): reserved for the exact hero character and the moving adoption pulse.
- **Robot Highlight** (`robot-highlight`): the single warm specular stop inside the authored robot-material wordmark state.
- **Signal Lime** (`lime`): official-logo continuity, focus rings, and guardrail accents.
- **Live Signal** (`signal-green`): the footer particle signal and rare high-energy states on dark fields.
- **Theatre Teal** (`theatre-teal`): the product-theatre emphasis color for capability headlines and measurement guides.
- **Training Mint** (`mint`): connective diagrams and protected-system surfaces.

### Neutral

- **Launch Paper** (`paper`): the opening, closing, and high-whitespace product fields.
- **Soft Workflow Paper** (`paper-soft`): briefing and diagram surfaces.
- **Operational Ink** (`ink`): primary text and near-black action surfaces.
- **Quiet Copy** (`muted`): secondary copy only; never placed on a colored field.
- **Structure Line** (`line`): hairline separators and UI boundaries.

**The Orange Is an Object Rule.** Robot orange belongs to the robot and its direct system pulse. It is not sprayed across buttons, headings, or decorative gradients.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Native neo-grotesque system stack, led by the platform's own UI family.

**Body Font:** The same system stack with calmer weight and spacing.

**Character:** Product-photography confidence without shipping proprietary fonts. Display letterspacing stops at the `-0.04em` readability floor; body copy stays direct and easy to scan.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (700, `clamp(3rem, 8vw, 8rem)`, 0.9): single-idea service and closing statements.
- **Headline** (700, `clamp(2.5rem, 5.7vw, 6rem)`, 0.95): section and briefing headlines.
- **Title** (700, `clamp(2rem, 4.8vw, 4.75rem)`, 0.98): highlight-panel statements.
- **Body** (400, `1rem`, 1.5): source-backed explanations, held to roughly 45–55 characters per line.
- **Label** (600, `0.875rem`, 1.2): navigation, tabs, and compact state labels.

**The Scale Carries the Drama Rule.** Large type, space, and weight create the hierarchy. Gradient text, ornamental serif pairings, and repeated uppercase eyebrow labels are prohibited.

**Launch-object scale:** The `WNF.AI` mark and the full-viewport persuasion statements intentionally exceed the ordinary 6rem content-heading ceiling because they function as the product object and chapter scenery, not dense reading text. Their responsive `clamp()` values are component-specific extensions of the display step; supporting copy remains inside the portable hierarchy.

## Layout

The page uses a full-bleed chapter model with content held inside a 90rem maximum canvas. Global navigation uses a centered `min(100% - 2rem, 90rem)` rail; theatrical sections use fluid page gutters that never fall below 1.5rem. Sticky scenes are allowed to own the viewport, but readable copy remains inside the safe canvas and inactive horizontal panels remove their copy before they cross the viewport edge.

Desktop compositions use asymmetrical two-column product staging: oversized copy owns one side while the robot, opening palm, editorial people, workflow, or interface proves the claim on the other. At 75rem and above, the full product spacing opens; below 64rem, capability lenses tighten around the robot; below 48rem, every scene becomes a vertical composition and motion distances collapse. The 320px floor is structural: no heading may depend on clipping, off-canvas translation, or hidden first characters to create scale.

The spacing rhythm comes from 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, and 128px steps. Product stages can exceed the ordinary section step only when the extra distance carries a scroll transition or protects an oversized object.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is flat by default. Depth comes from scale, occlusion, product imagery, sticky choreography, and tonal fields. Liquid-glass lenses are allowed only where they behave like controls, capability readouts, or literal interface surfaces; every lens uses a restrained highlight edge, backdrop blur, and one authored lift shadow.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Briefing ambient** (`0 24px 80px rgba(11, 24, 16, 0.12)`): only beneath the Morning Briefing interface.
- **Interactive lift** (`0 4px 24px rgba(11, 24, 16, 0.08)`): replay and small circular controls.
- **Glass lift** (`0 24px 72px rgba(11, 24, 16, 0.14)`): capability lenses, the active adoption step, and product-gallery panels.
- **Dark glass lift** (`0 32px 96px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.24)`): dark-field panels and the particle finale only.

### Layer Vocabulary

- **Base / content / product / scene:** `1 / 2 / 3 / 4` for local chapter composition.
- **Copy / mark / overlay / control:** `7 / 8 / 9 / 12` for the opening choreography.
- **Notice / header / skip:** `50 / 100 / 999` for global navigation and accessibility controls.

**The Flat-Until-Physical Rule.** A shadow is allowed only when an object is meant to feel physically above the page or responding to interaction.

## Shapes

The form language contrasts disciplined interface geometry with one friendly physical character. Controls, tabs, status labels, and microcards use full pills; interface shells use 16px corners; feature panels and glass lenses use the 28px large radius. The oversized liquid wordmark uses a proportional `0.12em` glyph radius so its glass edges scale with the type. The robot remains visibly squared and articulated, while orbits, measurement guides, particles, and adoption paths use circles and rounded line terminals.

Borders stay hairline and tonal except on the two authored people-photo stages, where a thick WNF signal edge and offset outline translate the approved Align industry-page framing grammar. Glass surfaces pair a soft white edge with backdrop blur; opaque product fields use either no border or the Structure Line token. Large sections are never rounded simply to imitate cards, and the opening hand is never clipped into a floating wrist or reused in the later human-led chapter.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** full pill (`999px`) with a 44px minimum target.
- **Primary:** Working Forest with white text and `12px 24px` padding.
- **Hover / Focus:** Operational Ink on hover; Signal Lime external focus ring; a short 0.97 press scale.
- **Text link:** Working Forest text with a directional arrow that moves only on pointer hover.

### Cards / Containers

- **Highlight panels:** distinct, horizontally sequenced product chapters with gently curved 28px corners. Only the centered panel exposes readable copy; neighbors remain quiet visual previews so text is never cut by the viewport.
- **Capability lenses:** translucent product-theatre readouts with one icon, one label, and one source-backed descriptor.
- **Briefing shell:** 16px radius, one hairline boundary, backdrop blur, and the ambient interface shadow.
- **Service chapters:** never cards; each owns a full viewport field.

### Navigation

The header is a translucent paper bar with the exact official logo, small direct labels, and one dark pill CTA. Mobile replaces labels with a two-line menu control and a full-screen native dialog.

### WNF.AI Opening

Five wordmark transformations run over 7.8 seconds: plain, circuit-wire, liquid glass, robot material, and shuffled compression. A product mark bridges the compression into the isolated robot, which arrives above the palm, swaps to a matched closed-eye image to blink, follows the pointer within a restrained range, and nods on activation. The button itself remains motion-stable; breathing motion lives inside its visual frame. Reduced-motion users receive the final complete state immediately.

### Robot Product Theatre

The orange character is treated like a product, not a mascot sticker. A sticky chapter moves from workflow/team measurement guides into four brand-specific capability lenses, then lifts the robot out of frame. Pointer tracking, blink states, and activation nods add personality while all essential meaning remains in source-backed text.

### Brand Icon System

Workflow, systems, team, policy, and adoption icons use a custom 48px line system with rounded terminals and circuit-node details derived from the official brain-and-circuit mark. Emoji, icon fonts, and generic library glyphs are prohibited.

Each icon has a job-specific motion signature: workflow travels, systems computes, team connects, policy guards, and adoption advances. Larger policy, systems, and human-led moments use orbital glass reactors instead of repeating the robot. Their moving nodes and circuit cores communicate an active system while preserving the robot as a scarce authored character.

### Particle Finale

The third and final robot is alpha-sampled as the particle source. One open-eye image is present in the footer seed; particles crossfade in only as that image fades out, so the robot never appears doubled. Its orange silhouette charges, dissolves into deterministic points, travels through six circuit lanes, changes into the official green-and-gray palette, and settles against alpha-sampled target coordinates from the exact logo. The source image stays hidden during travel and appears only after the particles lock to the same stable pixels. A signal-meter button replays the sequence; the canvas pauses outside the viewport, provides a static fallback, and is omitted for reduced-motion users.

### Editorial People Stages

Training & Policy uses a real three-person workshop divided by three liquid-glass apertures. Human Control uses a real advisor and business leader working side by side, with no floating hand and no additional robot. Both scenes use directional documentary light, deterministic grain, a thick WNF outline, one finite traveling refraction, and cursor-reactive depth; reduced-motion mode removes the scan and tilt while preserving the complete image.

### Morning Briefing

Native ARIA tabs expose Needs You, Follow-ups, and Today. Arrow keys, Home, and End move selection. Every row uses exact source copy; the interface never implies a live connected account.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** preserve the five approved wordmark transformations, product bridge, and measured 7.8-second pacing.
- **Do** use the official logo, exact current homepage copy, and traceable routes.
- **Do** keep the human visibly in control of the AI collaborator.
- **Do** keep the robot to exactly three placements: opening, product theatre, and footer transformation.
- **Do** use single-idea viewports and generous whitespace instead of shrinking everything into a dashboard.
- **Do** keep every active text block fully inside the viewport at 320px through wide desktop sizes.
- **Do** use liquid glass only for interactive or informational lenses and keep clickable motion on an inner visual layer.
- **Do** load the complete final hero immediately for reduced-motion users.

### Don't:

- **Don't** make this look like a generic AI SaaS template, a neon cyberpunk robot page, or a card-filled agency theme.
- **Don't** copy Apple trademarks, Apple product media, Apple copy, Apple pricing, or Apple navigation branding.
- **Don't** use gradient text outside the single robot-material opening state, generic purple-blue gradients, emoji icons, fake dashboards, or invented metrics.
- **Don't** invent clients, results, testimonials, guarantees, or product capabilities.
- **Don't** use a colored side stripe, nest cards, or hide essential meaning behind hover or animation.
- **Don't** repeat the robot as a decorative icon; use the branded circuit reactors for policy, systems, and human-led states.
