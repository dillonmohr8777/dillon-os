The primary datastore is the largest remaining gap. Redis and Vector are a cache and an index, not a system of record, and a product with approval queues across 120,000 tenants has one. One read of their subprocessor list would settle it.

The lineage explains the shape

The private monorepo is still called bti-chat, and the encryption library's key-derivation strings are literally bti-e2ee-salt and bti-e2ee-key. BuildThatIdea (Feb 2025, a no-code GPT-wrapper builder) became Okara Chat (Dec 2025) became Okara AI CMO (Mar 2026). One application, three product skins.

The part worth stealing

Their SEO-to-code loop is genuinely good: the SEO agent finds a technical issue, spins up a disposable sandbox, runs the analysis in isolation, hands findings to a coding agent, and that agent opens a pull request against the customer's repo with diffs and explanations inline. Fixes needing structural change are flagged manual rather than attempted. Most competitors cannot do this at all.

Two published analyses of okara, both wrong in opposite directions

One files okara as a “cron plus prompt templates” reference implementation — “task-specific prompt templates marketed as agents.” Too dismissive: that category explicitly means no workflow knows about any other, which cannot describe a system needing queue retries, flow control, and a shared state layer.

Another claims a “multi-agent swarm” with a task-routing brain, a unified memory pool, and ~40 models — asserting it was “confirmed” while citing no artifacts. It is inference from the marketing surface.

The accurate label, from first-party evidence: a queue-driven worker fleet with shared KV state and a vector-backed dedup filter, plus sandboxed code execution and a human-in-the-loop PR gate. No trace of LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, Inngest, Trigger.dev or Temporal — QStash is the orchestrator.

03The gap that decided everything

Not a missing feature — a missing property

Architectural finding

QStash gives at-least-once delivery, not durable execution. There is no run journal.

A retry re-runs a step. It does not resume a half-finished agent run with its intermediate reasoning, retrieved documents, and partial tool results intact.

So for a product whose entire interaction model is approving agent output that will post under a customer's name, this question has no answer:

Which model, which prompt version, which retrieved page, and which human click produced this reply?

At 120,000 tenants that is a brand-safety and legal exposure. It also makes regression testing impossible: there is no recording to replay a prompt change against.

Five more, briefly

  • Serverless is the wrong shape for long agent runs. Request-scoped execution forces agent work into artificial steps that re-hydrate context on each hop — so the brand-voice document goes to the model again every time. Latency, correctness and cost problem at once.
  • No visible caching strategy at 4B tokens/day. The per-run context is exactly the reusable kind. Provider prompt caching on that stable prefix is the largest single cost lever available.
  • Tenant isolation lives in application code. State in Redis means isolation is a key-prefix convention; one prefix bug is a cross-tenant leak. And they hold write-scoped OAuth tokens for 120,000 sites.
  • Flow Control throttles globally, not per tenant. It keeps okara under a platform's rate limit. It does not stop one heavy tenant consuming the shared quota, and a shared app credential tripping abuse detection kills the flagship feature for everyone at once.
  • “New models the same day they ship” has no eval gate. Their proudest capability is also their biggest quality risk.
Why not Temporal

The obvious fix for “no durable execution” is to adopt Temporal or Inngest. That is the wrong call here for a specific reason: this product independently needs a per-run audit record — input snapshot, prompt hash, model, tokens, cost, sources. That record already is a step journal.

Adopt a framework and you build the audit journal anyway, then maintain a second opaque vendor journal beside it. Owning it gives replay, audit, cost accounting and the approval queue from one source of truth, with one stateful dependency — and sidesteps determinism constraints, the largest source of durable-execution incidents.

The exploitable seam

The marketing says “puts marketing on autopilot.” The documentation says you click publish on every post, Reddit never auto-posts, and Hacker News never auto-posts. Every reviewer who actually paid lands on co-pilot, not autopilot.

Three whole categories are absent, and okara's own comparison content concedes the first: paid media management, local SEO and Google Business Profile, and reconciling platform-reported conversions against real leads. Local appears nowhere in its roster — or in any GEO tool.

Pricing, and why its instability matters

Four headline prices for the AI CMO are simultaneously indexed — almost certainly sequential increases rather than tiers: $99/mo (2,000 credits, the most-documented), $129/mo on their own influencer page, $249/mo in their own docs, and a $20/mo tier from an earlier structure.

The free tier is reported as both 5 and 20 one-time credits, so it has been re-cut. Top-up packs cost roughly what the subscription does, which makes a heavy month $150–280 rather than $99.

$99 → $129 → $249 inside roughly a year, on a product whose free tier already reads as a demo, is the pricing history of a company still looking for its number.

The complaint that recurs most: credit opacity
“Zero transparency on how many credits one CMO initialization actually burns.”

The paid plan carries 2,000 credits and okara's own documentation reportedly puts a full-output month at ~1,700 — about 300 of headroom. There is no published per-action credit table. One user reported credits gone in fourteen days.

Alongside it: “no public API, no MCP server, and no Zapier or Make hooks, so you cannot orchestrate its agents from your own stack”, and no documented bulk export — “leaving means walking away from the accumulated context.”

The measurement point worth isolating

During the build, the weighted confidence interval collapsed to zero width at a presence rate of zero — reporting 0.0% [0.0–0.0] across 54 runs. That is precisely the false precision the whole measurement engine exists to prevent, appearing in the engine itself. Fifty-four runs of nothing is consistent with a true rate of several percent, so it now reports 0% [0–6.6%].

Separately, the control cohort caught the simulator naming the client in “what is the best cordless drill?” — a question it has no business appearing in. Both are fixed and both are tested. A control cohort that never catches anything isn't a control.

06Where okara is ahead

Stated plainly, because a teardown that finds only weaknesses isn't a teardown

For four engineers serving 120,000 tenants, most of their choices are close to optimal. The AI Gateway deleted eight provider integrations and all retry logic from a codebase two people maintain. A managed queue instead of standing up Kafka is right. A managed vector index instead of a Pinecone bill is right. None of that should be “improved” by adding operational surface.

And on capability: okara actually publishes to five CMS platforms today. Its sandbox-to-pull-request loop for technical SEO is a real, non-trivial capability. Its client-side encryption is competently engineered, and its whitepaper honestly discloses that messages are plaintext during active processing. It has 120,000 users.

The build that followed this teardown has publishing paths specified, risk-tiered and idempotency-keyed — and no publisher implemented. It has one demo workspace. It has never made a live model call, because the environment it was built in had no API key. Those are stated in its README rather than glossed, for the same reason every number in it carries a source.
